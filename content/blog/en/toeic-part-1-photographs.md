**TOEIC Part 1** is the shortest part of the test: six photographs, four spoken statements for each, and one answer to choose. The statements are played once, they are not printed in the test book, and you must choose the one that best describes the picture. It looks easy, but the options are written to trap people who listen for one word. This guide explains the four kinds of photo, the tricks behind wrong answers, the vocabulary that repeats, and a practice plan.

## How Part 1 works

You see a photograph in your test book and hear four statements labelled A to D. Only the photograph is printed. Choose the statement that best describes what you see. There are six questions, and they are the first six of the Listening section; the full layout of the test is in [the TOEIC test format guide](/en/blog/toeic-test-format/). Because everything is spoken once, your preparation has to include listening and looking at the same time.

Part 1 is worth only six of 200 questions, but the vocabulary repeats, which makes it a good place to build confidence. The same listening habits help later in [Part 2](/en/blog/toeic-listening-part-2-tactics/) and in [Parts 3 and 4](/en/blog/toeic-part-3-and-4-strategies/).

## Four kinds of photograph

{{diagram: d1 | Decide the kind of photograph first; it tells you which grammar to expect}}

**One person.** The statements describe an action or a state: *The man is reading a document*, *The woman is wearing a hat*. Look at the hands, the objects and the direction of the eyes.

**Two or more people.** Statements may describe a shared action (*They are shaking hands*) or one person within a group (*One of the women is pointing at a screen*). Check that the statement is true for everyone it mentions.

**Objects and places.** There are no people, or they are far away. Statements describe positions: *Chairs are arranged around a table*, *A bicycle is leaning against a wall*.

**Scenes.** Streets, offices, ports and stations. Statements may mention several things: *Vehicles are parked along the road*.

## A quick routine for each photograph

{{diagram: d2 | Use the short pause before the statements to prepare your ears}}

When the directions for Part 1 are read, you can look at the first picture. For each photo:

1. **Find the main subject**, such as a person, a group or an object.
2. **Note the action or position**: holding, standing, stacked, hanging.
3. **Predict some verbs and nouns** you might hear.
4. **Listen to all four statements**, even if A sounds right. Another statement may fit better.
5. **Choose the best match**, mark it, and look at the next photo.

## The traps behind wrong answers

| Trap | Example for a photo of a woman at a desk | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong subject | *The man is writing a letter.* | There is no man |
| Wrong action | *She is talking on the phone.* | She is typing |
| Similar sound | *The files are being stacked.* | The tiles, not files, are stacked |
| Wrong tense or state | *The computer has been turned off.* | The screen is on |
| True but not visible | *The woman is waiting for a client.* | You cannot see that |
| Partly true | *She is typing on a laptop in a park.* | The location is wrong |

A statement must be completely true and describe what is visible. Do not choose an option because you think something might be happening.

## A worked example

Imagine a photograph of two people in an office. A man in a jacket is standing beside a desk and handing a folder to a seated woman, who is looking at a computer screen. You hear four statements:

- A: *The woman is signing a form.* She is looking at a screen, not signing, so this is wrong.
- B: *The man is handing a folder to the woman.* This matches the action you can see, and it says nothing that the photograph does not show.
- C: *They are both seated at a table.* Only the woman is seated, so this is partly true but wrong.
- D: *A folder has been left on the floor.* The folder is in the man's hand, so this fails.

B is the answer. Notice that you could only be certain after hearing all four, because statement A and C each contain a detail that sounds plausible. This is why you should never choose the first statement that seems right.

## Grammar you will hear

Part 1 repeats a small set of structures:

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Present continuous (action) | *The people are crossing the street.* |
| Present continuous passive (action by someone) | *A table is being set.* |
| Present perfect passive (result) | *The boxes have been stacked.* |
| Present simple passive (state) | *Several lamps are lit.* |
| There is / there are | *There are plants beside the window.* |

The difference between *is being set* (someone is doing it now) and *has been set* (it is finished) is one of the most frequent traps. If nobody in the photo is setting the table, *a table is being set* is wrong, but *a table has been set* or *the table is set for dinner* may fit. A good review of the structures is in the blog guide to [passive voice in English](/en/blog/passive-voice-in-english/).

## Vocabulary that repeats

Learn verbs and nouns in groups. A few groups are worth knowing by heart:

- **Hands and objects:** holding, carrying, pushing, pulling, pointing, reaching for, putting away.
- **Clothing and posture:** wearing, putting on, kneeling, bending over, leaning against, facing.
- **Places and positions:** along, beside, across from, in front of, behind, on top of, stacked, lined up.
- **Workplace items:** a counter, a shelf, a cart, a ladder, a screen, a document, a drawer.

Most of these words appear again in the Listening and Reading sections. If you like to learn from the dictionary, search a word such as [shelf](/app/#/dict/shelf) and add it to your review list. For general workplace vocabulary, see [daily English conversation sentences](/en/blog/daily-english-conversation-sentences-guide/).

## A ten-day practice plan

| Days | Task |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Learn the verb groups; describe five photos from your life out loud in the present continuous |
| 3–4 | Practise passive forms: *is being* versus *has been* |
| 5–6 | Do Part 1 sets and write down the trap in each wrong answer |
| 7–8 | Add place and position vocabulary; describe the same photo three ways |
| 9 | Do a timed set of six photographs |
| 10 | Review every error, then move on to Part 2 |

A habit that helps: look at any photo in a news site or on your phone and say two true sentences about it, then one false sentence that sounds true. This trains you to spot traps.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions are in TOEIC Part 1?

There are six photograph questions at the start of the Listening section. Each one has four spoken statements and you choose one.

### Are the statements printed in the test book?

No. Only the photographs are printed. You hear the statements once, so careful listening matters.

### Is Part 1 easy to improve?

For many learners it is. The vocabulary and grammar repeat, so regular practice with photographs and passive forms can raise accuracy quickly. Results depend on your starting level.

### What should I do if I am unsure?

Eliminate statements that clearly contradict the photograph, choose the best remaining one and move on. Do not stay on one question while the next audio begins.
