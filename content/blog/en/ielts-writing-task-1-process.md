An **IELTS Writing Task 1 process** question gives you a diagram showing how something is made or how something happens in nature, and asks you to describe it in at least 150 words. There are no trends to compare and no data to select. The challenge is language: you must describe a sequence clearly, use the passive voice naturally and vary your linking words so that the report does not become a list of *then... then... then*. This guide shows how to read a process, write an overview, and use sequencing language, with an original model.

## Two kinds of IELTS Writing Task 1 process

{{diagram: d2 | Natural cycles and man-made processes use slightly different grammar}}

A **man-made process** shows how people produce something, such as bricks, paper or recycled glass. The grammar is mostly present simple passive: *the bottles are collected, then they are sorted*. A **natural process** shows a cycle, such as the water cycle or the life of a butterfly. It often uses present simple active (*the larva grows*) as well as passive forms. Check which type you have before you write.

## Step 1: understand the sequence

Look at the title, then count the stages. Find the starting point and the end product. If it is a cycle, there is no real end, so say that the process repeats. Notice any inputs (raw materials), outputs (products or waste) and who or what performs each step: machines, people or natural forces.

Process questions appear in the Academic test. Do not worry about explaining the science. If the diagram says *crushed*, write *crushed*; you do not need to explain how a machine works.

## Step 2: write the overview

The overview is one or two sentences about the whole process: how many stages, where it starts and where it ends. Original overview: *Overall, the recycling of glass involves six main stages, starting with the collection of used bottles and ending with the delivery of new glass products.* No details are needed here. The guide to [IELTS Writing mistakes](/en/blog/ielts-writing-mistakes/) explains why a missing overview costs marks in every Task 1 type.

## An original process

*The diagram shows how used glass bottles are recycled into new glass products. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features.*

{{diagram: d1 | The six stages, from collection to cooling and delivery}}

## A model report

> The diagram illustrates how used glass bottles are turned into new glass products.
>
> Overall, the process has six stages, beginning with the collection of bottles and ending with the delivery of the finished items to shops. The glass is reused rather than thrown away, so old bottles become new products.
>
> In the first stage, used bottles are gathered at recycling points. They are then sorted by colour, so that the glass of each colour is handled separately. Next, the sorted glass is crushed by machine into small pieces, and in the following stage these pieces are carried to a furnace, where they are melted at a very high temperature.
>
> After that, the molten glass is poured into moulds, where it is formed into new bottles and jars. Finally, the new products are cooled, and the finished items are sent to shops, which completes the process. In short, each stage prepares the glass for the next, moving from collection through sorting, crushing and melting to moulding and delivery.

The report has about 167 words, a comfortable margin above the 150-word minimum.

## Reading a diagram quickly

Most candidates lose time by staring at the picture. Use a fixed routine: put your pencil on the first box and say the verb silently (*collect*), move to the next arrow and say the next verb (*sort*), and so on until you reach the end. Write the verbs on the question paper in order. You now have the skeleton of the report. Then check each arrow for extra information, such as a label, a machine name or a condition (*if the glass is clean*). Add only those details.

## Why it works

- **Each stage is described once**, in order, with one main verb.
- **Passive voice is used where the agent does not matter**: *bottles are collected*, *glass is crushed*.
- **Sequencing words vary**: *in the first stage*, *then*, *next*, *after that*, *subsequently*, *finally*.
- **A reason or purpose is added only if the diagram supports it** (add one only if the diagram shows it).
- **There is a clear overview** that counts the stages.

## The passive voice you need

A process report uses the passive constantly. The form is *to be* plus the past participle: *is melted*, *are sorted*, *has been completed*. If you want a full review, read [passive voice in English](/en/blog/passive-voice-in-english/) and [passive voice rules and examples](/en/blog/passive-voice-in-english-rules-and-examples/).

| Active | Passive for a process |
|---|---|
| Workers collect the bottles. | The bottles are collected. |
| A machine crushes the glass. | The glass is crushed. |
| The factory delivers the products. | The products are delivered. |

Use the active voice when the agent matters or when a natural process describes what something does: *the water evaporates*, *the larva eats leaves*. Mixing active and passive is natural; forcing everything into the passive is not.

## Language for sequence

| Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|
| Start | *To begin with, At the first stage, The process begins when...* |
| Continue | *Next, Then, After that, Following this, Subsequently* |
| Show two things at once | *Meanwhile, At the same time, While this is happening* |
| Finish | *Finally, At the last stage, The process ends with* |
| Purpose | *in order to, so that, which allows* |
| Condition | *once, as soon as, until, when* |

Do not overuse *then*. For more variety read [linking words for IELTS writing](/en/blog/linking-words-for-ielts-writing/).

## Common mistakes

1. **Writing a paragraph per stage.** Group two or three stages in a paragraph.
2. **Ignoring the overview.** State the number of stages and the beginning and end.
3. **Active verbs for machines.** *A machine crushes the glass* is correct, but constant active forms make the report sound like a story. Use the passive for most steps.
4. **Adding information that is not in the diagram.** Do not explain science that is not shown.
5. **Copying labels.** Use verbs and nouns from the labels, but form complete sentences.
6. **Tense errors.** A process is normally described in the present simple. Only use a past tense if the diagram refers to a past time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to explain why each stage happens?

Only if the diagram makes the reason clear. Describe what happens at each stage; explanations that are not shown may count as invented information.

### Should I use the passive voice throughout?

Use it for most steps, but vary with active forms when the subject matters or for natural cycles. Over-using one structure can limit your grammatical range.

### How many paragraphs should a process report have?

Three or four: an introduction, an overview, and one or two detail paragraphs depending on the number of stages.

### What if there are more than eight stages?

Group related stages. You do not need to mention every small step, but you should cover the whole sequence from start to end.
