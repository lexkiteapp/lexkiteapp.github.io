In Australia's General Skilled Migration points grid, English can add 10 or 20 points, which is comparable to the 5 points (subclass 190) or 15 points (subclass 491) a state nomination adds. Knowing which IELTS score for Australia PR earns those points tells you what to aim for before you book.

The Australian Department of Home Affairs ([immi.homeaffairs.gov.au](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-support/meeting-our-requirements/english-language)) classifies English proficiency into strict statutory tiers: Competent, Proficient, and Superior. Since 7 August 2025 Home Affairs applies the English requirements to each skill separately and no longer accepts at-home tests such as IELTS Online. Unlike university admission standards, which often accept an overall band average, the IELTS levels below are read component by component. A dazzling 8.5 in Listening will not salvage a 6.5 in Writing; your tier is determined solely by your lowest individual score.

## Which IELTS score for Australia PR earns which points

Points-tested skilled visas—including the Skilled Independent visa (subclass 189), Skilled Nominated visa (subclass 190), and Skilled Work Regional visa (subclass 491)—award points based on your verified test results at the time of invitation.

{{diagram: d1 | Australian Department of Home Affairs English tiers and migration points.}}

- **Competent English (0 points):** IELTS 6.0 in each of the four components. It is the baseline for an Expression of Interest (EOI) for subclasses 189, 190 and 491.
- **Proficient English (10 points):** 7.0 in each component. Ten points can separate profiles with the same total.
- **Superior English (20 points):** 8.0 in each component. Check the latest published SkillSelect invitation results for your occupation to see how much those points matter.
- **Passport shortcut:** Holders of a passport from the UK, the USA, Canada, New Zealand or the Republic of Ireland can meet the English requirement without a test; confirm on the Home Affairs page which level this covers.

The other tests Home Affairs accepts, and their scores for each level, are compared in our [PTE vs IELTS for Australia](/en/blog/pte-vs-ielts-australia/) article.

## The single lowest component rule in Australian points assessment

The most painful setback candidates face is the "all four components" rule. Consider this example:

| Component | Candidate Score | Required for Superior | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Listening** | 8.5 | 8.0 | Passed |
| **Reading** | 8.5 | 8.0 | Passed |
| **Speaking** | 8.0 | 8.0 | Passed |
| **Writing** | 7.5 | 8.0 | **Missed by 0.5** |
| **Overall Band** | **8.0** | - | **Downgraded to Proficient (10 pts)** |

Despite securing an overall band of 8.0, this applicant receives only 10 points instead of 20. That missing 0.5 in Writing costs a full 10 migration points. Task 2 argument and structure—see our [IELTS Writing Task 2 structure guide](/en/blog/ielts-writing-task-2-structure/) and the [IELTS Writing model answers hub](/en/samples/)—is where many candidates lose that last half band.

## Planning backwards from your lodgement date

Because the three-year window runs from the test date, work out when you expect to lodge your EOI or accept an invitation and count back. A result that is comfortably inside the window on that day gives you room to retake a single weak skill without losing the others, but only if your visa accepts that route, so read the conditions first. If your practice scores sit at 7.0 in three skills and 6.5 in one, the gap between Proficient and Competent in that one skill is the thing to fix, because the level follows the lowest component.

It also helps to decide early which level you are really targeting. Competent English is the minimum, Proficient is worth 10 points and Superior 20. Candidates whose occupation is competitive often aim higher, while others reach their points total in other ways, so compare your own points calculation with recent SkillSelect results before you commit months of study to an 8.0 target.

## Subclass differences: 189, 190 and 491

Language score requirements interact differently across Australian visa subclasses:

1. **Subclass 189 (Skilled Independent):** Entirely points-driven. Because there is no nomination bonus (unlike the 5 points for 190 or 15 points for 491), English points matter more.
2. **Subclass 190 & 491 (State Nominated & Regional):** Home Affairs requires Competent English as the baseline, and a state or territory may set its own language requirements for its nomination lists. Read the nominating state's page for your occupation, because language results can matter when a state decides whom to nominate.
3. **Employer-sponsored visas** (such as 482 Skills in Demand and 186) have their own English rules, which are not points-tested. Read the visa page for your stream. This article covers the points-tested 189, 190 and 491 only.

## Test validity: how old can your test result be?

For tests taken from 7 August 2025, Home Affairs accepts results for up to **three years from the test date**. Do not confuse that with the two-year validity IELTS prints on its own result form, which is a different rule for other purposes.

Home Affairs sets the exact rule per visa, so on the day you plan to lodge your EOI or accept an invitation, open the English page of the Home Affairs site and check that the result is still within its validity.

## Preparation strategy: moving from Band 7.0 to Band 8.0

Bridging the gap between Proficient (7.0) and Superior (8.0) requires shifting from passive comprehension to active structural control:

1. **Reading precision:** In General Training Reading, Band 8.0 means roughly 37 to 38 correct answers out of 40 (Academic needs fewer), so you cannot afford careless scanning errors. Review our techniques on [skimming and scanning for IELTS Reading](/en/blog/ielts-reading-skimming-and-scanning/).
2. **Writing coherence:** An 8.0 in Writing requires effortless paragraph transitions, precise lexical range, and sophisticated discourse control. Review our guide on [linking words for IELTS writing](/en/blog/linking-words-for-ielts-writing/) to avoid mechanical signposting.
3. **Speaking fluency:** Band 8.0 speakers demonstrate unhesitating discourse with idiomatic command. Familiarise yourself with extended turn strategies in our [IELTS Speaking Part 2 guide](/en/blog/ielts-speaking-part-2-tips/).
4. **General vs Academic:** Make sure you sit the correct format permitted by your assessing authority; see our breakdown of [IELTS General vs Academic for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-general-vs-academic-immigration/).

A steady routine helps here: reviewing collocations and vocabulary a little every day makes complex structures easier to produce in both Writing and Speaking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Australia accept IELTS One Skill Retake for PR?

Home Affairs' accepted-test list names IELTS One Skill Retake, but check the conditions for your subclass and test date on its English page before you book a retake. Your skills assessing authority may have its own view too.

### Can I combine scores from two separate IELTS test attempts?

No. Each skill must reach the level in one result, so scores from two separate sittings cannot be merged unless Home Affairs says your visa accepts One Skill Retake. PTE results cannot be mixed with IELTS either; see the PTE comparison linked above.

### Is General Training accepted for an Australian PR points claim?

Yes. The Department of Home Affairs accepts both IELTS General Training and Academic for points claims under subclasses 189, 190, and 491. Always confirm your assessing authority's guidelines first.
