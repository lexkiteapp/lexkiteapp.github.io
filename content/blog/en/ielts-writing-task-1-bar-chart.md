An **IELTS Writing Task 1 bar chart** asks you to describe data in at least 150 words in about 20 minutes. You are not asked for an opinion or an explanation of why the figures changed; you must select the key features, group them sensibly and report them accurately. The report works best when it follows a simple plan: introduction, overview, then two detail paragraphs. This guide gives the method, an original model answer built on invented data, the language you need and the mistakes that most often cost marks.

## What the examiner wants to see

Task 1 in the Academic test is marked on four criteria: task achievement, coherence and cohesion, lexical resource, and grammatical range and accuracy. For a chart, task achievement means three things: you describe the most important features, you include an overview, and you report the numbers correctly. Writing Task 1 is worth one third of the Writing score, Task 2 two thirds, so keep to the time limit. If you also practise line graphs, the guide to [IELTS line graph writing](/en/blog/ielts-writing-task-1-line-graph/) covers trends over time.

## The four-step method for an IELTS Writing Task 1 bar chart

{{diagram: d1 | Plan the report as four short paragraphs}}

1. **Read the title and axes (1 minute).** What is measured? What units? What time or categories? Mistakes here, such as writing *people* instead of *percentage*, damage everything after.
2. **Find the key features (2 minutes).** Look for the highest, the lowest, the biggest change and any pattern or exception. Write two or three short notes.
3. **Group the data (1 minute).** Decide how to divide the details between two paragraphs, for example by highest and lowest values, or by year.
4. **Write and check (16 minutes).** Write in the order shown for about 14 minutes, then use two minutes to check numbers and verb forms.

## The data: an original example

Here is an invented chart described as a table. The question is: *The chart shows the percentage of households with internet access in four countries in 2010 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features.*

| Country | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|
| Country A | 60 | 92 |
| Country B | 45 | 80 |
| Country C | 25 | 65 |
| Country D | 70 | 85 |

## Model report

> The bar chart compares the proportion of homes with an internet connection in four countries in 2010 and 2020.
>
> Overall, access rose in every country over the decade, and the country that started highest grew the least. Country A had the highest figure in 2020, while Country C remained the lowest.
>
> In 2010, Country D led with 70 per cent, followed by Country A at 60 per cent. Country B stood at 45 per cent, and only a quarter of households in Country C were connected. Country D grew the least over the period, reaching 85 per cent by 2020.
>
> By contrast, the other three countries expanded sharply. Country A climbed by 32 percentage points to 92 per cent and overtook Country D, Country B rose by 35 points to 80 per cent, and Country C more than doubled, from 25 to 65 per cent, which was the largest proportional increase.

The report has about 150 words, which meets the minimum. It does not mention reasons such as cost or technology because the chart does not give them.

## Why this report works

- **The introduction paraphrases the question.** *Households with internet access* became *homes with an internet connection*.
- **The overview contains the trend and a comparison**, and it contains no numbers beyond the key idea.
- **The details are grouped** instead of describing each country one after another.
- **The figures are accurate and units are clear.** Percentage points are used for differences, per cent for values.
- **The language is varied**: *rose*, *climbed*, *overtook*, *remained*, *led*.

## Three overview sentences to adapt

The overview is the paragraph candidates most often forget, so practise it on its own. Here are three original patterns, each tied to a different kind of chart:

- **A change over time:** *Overall, the figures rose in every group, although the pace of growth differed.*
- **A comparison at one point:** *Overall, the largest share was found in the first category, while the smallest was recorded in the last.*
- **A mixed pattern:** *While most categories increased, one remained almost unchanged, and this exception stands out.*

An overview should contain no detailed numbers. Its job is to give the reader the shape of the data in one or two sentences, so that the later paragraphs fill in the details. If you are unsure what the main feature is, ask yourself: *if I had ten seconds to tell a friend about this chart, what would I say?* Then write that sentence. Finally, check that your overview does not contradict your details; an examiner will compare them.

## Language you can reuse

Take care with prepositions: *rose from 25 to 65*, *an increase of 40 points*, *at 70 per cent*, *by 32 percentage points*. For more variety in your sentences, see [complex sentences for IELTS writing](/en/blog/complex-sentences-for-ielts-writing/). To make the links between sentences natural, use the connectors in [linking words for IELTS writing](/en/blog/linking-words-for-ielts-writing/).

### Choosing the right tense

Tense follows time. A chart about 2010 and 2020 uses the past simple (*rose*, *stood at*). A chart that shows a forecast uses *is expected to* or *will*. A chart without any time reference, such as a comparison of survey results, uses the present simple (*the largest share goes to*). If the chart mixes past and future years, switch tense at the right point and check each sentence on your final read-through.

## Common mistakes in bar chart reports

| Mistake | Why it costs marks | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No overview | Task achievement is limited | Write one clear paragraph for the main trends |
| Listing every number | The report becomes a copy of the chart | Select and group key figures |
| Giving reasons for changes | The data does not explain them | Describe only what the chart shows |
| Wrong tense | Past data needs past forms | Match tenses to the time reference |
| Confusing per cent and percentage points | Inaccurate reporting | Use points for differences |
| Under 150 words | Penalty on task achievement | Aim for 160 or more |

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should an IELTS Task 1 bar chart answer be?

At least 150 words. Around 160 to 190 is a comfortable length that gives you room to describe key figures without rushing.

### Do I need to write an overview?

Yes. A clear overview of the main trends or differences is one of the most important parts of a high-scoring Task 1 report. Place it after the introduction.

### Should I describe every bar?

No. Select the most important features and group the others. Describing every bar in order shows that you copied the chart rather than analysed it.

### Can I give my opinion or reasons?

No. Task 1 is about reporting information, so describe what the chart shows and leave out explanations that are not in the data.
