The **TOEIC test format** for Listening & Reading is simple to summarize: 200 multiple-choice questions in about two hours, split into a Listening section (about 45 minutes, 100 questions, Parts 1–4) and a Reading section (75 minutes, 100 questions, Parts 5–7). Your score ranges from 10 to 990. Knowing exactly what each part tests, and how long you can afford to spend on it, is the fastest way to stop losing points to the clock rather than to your English.

(TOEIC is a trademark of its owner, ETS. Lexkite is not affiliated with ETS, and all practice questions below are our own.)

## TOEIC test format at a glance

| Section | Part | Task | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening (~45 min) | 1 | Photographs | 6 |
| | 2 | Question–response | 25 |
| | 3 | Conversations | 39 |
| | 4 | Talks | 30 |
| Reading (75 min) | 5 | Incomplete sentences | 30 |
| | 6 | Text completion | 16 |
| | 7 | Reading comprehension | 54 |
| **Total** | | | **200** |

All content is set in workplace and everyday situations: offices, travel, shopping, meetings, schedules, emails and announcements. You do not need specialist business knowledge, but you do need business vocabulary. This guide covers the Listening & Reading test; TOEIC Speaking & Writing is a separate test.

## Listening: Parts 1–4

In the Listening section you hear each recording **only once**, and the audio controls the pace. You cannot go back.

### Part 1 – Photographs (6 questions)

You see a photo and hear four statements; choose the one that best describes it. The statements are not printed.

*Example (original):* a photo of a woman watering plants on a balcony. Correct: *She's tending to some plants.* A typical trap: *She's planting a tree.* It uses a related word but describes a different action.

**Tip:** pay close attention to verbs and to the difference between an action in progress (*is being loaded*) and a resulting state (*has been loaded*).

### Part 2 – Question–response (25 questions)

You hear a question or statement and three possible responses; nothing is printed. Choose the most natural reply.

*Example (original):* "When is the budget meeting?" (A) *In the conference room.* (B) *It's been moved to Thursday.* (C) *Yes, I met him.* The answer is (B). (A) answers *where*, and (C) answers a yes/no question that was never asked.

**Tip:** catch the first word (*who, when, where, why, how*). Indirect answers such as *I'll check the schedule* are often correct.

### Part 3 – Conversations (39 questions)

You hear short conversations between two or three speakers, each followed by three questions with printed answer options. Some sets include a simple graphic such as a schedule or price list.

**Tip:** read the three questions *before* the audio starts so you know what to listen for. Answers usually come in the order of the questions. Watch for "implied meaning" questions, which quote a short line from the audio (*What does the woman mean when she says, "That's a first"?*). They test tone and context, not vocabulary, so listen to the sentence before and after the quoted line.

### Part 4 – Talks (30 questions)

One speaker gives an announcement, voicemail, tour introduction or short presentation, followed by three questions.

**Tip:** the first sentence usually reveals the purpose and the speaker's role, which answers at least one question. Many learners find fast natural speech the hardest part; our guide to [connected speech](/en/blog/connected-speech-in-english/) explains why words seem to disappear in real recordings.

## Reading: Parts 5–7

### Part 5 – Incomplete sentences (30 questions)

Choose the word or phrase that completes a sentence. Questions test word forms, verb tenses, prepositions, conjunctions and vocabulary.

*Example (original):* "The new software will be installed ___ all employees have logged off." (A) during (B) once (C) despite (D) because of. Answer: (B) *once*, because a full clause follows, so a conjunction is needed.

### Part 6 – Text completion (16 questions)

Short texts such as emails or notices, each with four gaps. One gap per text asks you to choose a whole sentence that fits, so you must follow the logic of the passage, not just the grammar of one line.

### Part 7 – Reading comprehension (54 questions)

Single passages and multiple passages (double and triple), including emails, articles, forms, text message chains and advertisements. Questions ask about main ideas, details, inferences and vocabulary in context. In multiple-passage sets, some answers require combining information from two texts. If you want to understand how professional emails are built, which helps here, see [how to write a formal email](/en/blog/how-to-write-a-formal-email/).

## Managing time in the Reading section

Running out of time in Part 7 is the single most common complaint about the TOEIC. A practical plan for 75 minutes:

| Part | Suggested time | Pace |
|---|---|---|
| Part 5 | 10–12 min | about 20–25 seconds per question |
| Part 6 | 8–10 min | about 2–2.5 minutes per text |
| Part 7 | 53–57 min | about 1 minute per question |

Rules that protect your score:

- **Don't get stuck.** If a Part 5 question takes more than 30 seconds, choose your best guess and move on.
- **Never leave a blank.** Your score is based on the number of correct answers, so a guess can only help.
- **Read the questions first in Part 7,** then scan the passage for the information each one needs.

## How TOEIC scores work

Listening and Reading are each scored from 5 to 495, giving a total from 10 to 990. The scaled score is based on the number of questions you answer correctly; there is no penalty for wrong answers. Because scaling varies between test forms, there is no fixed "X correct answers = Y points" table you can rely on.

There is no pass or fail. Employers and universities set their own requirements, so always check the specific target you need.

## Paper or computer, and what happens on test day

The Listening & Reading test is offered on paper at many test centers and, in some countries, on computer or as a remote test at home; check what your local ETS representative offers. On the paper version you mark answers on a separate answer sheet, and the Listening audio runs continuously, so you cannot pause to transfer answers. Each part opens with spoken directions that never change from test to test. Once you know them, use those seconds to preview the next photos or questions instead of listening to instructions you already understand.

## Typical traps in each part

Every part of the test has its own favorite way of catching test takers out. Recognizing the pattern is half the battle.

| Part | Common trap | How to beat it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A word from the photo used in the wrong action | Check the verb, not just the nouns |
| 2 | An option that repeats a word from the question | Similar sound or same word is often a distractor |
| 3 | The first speaker's plan changes later | Wait for words like *actually* or *instead* |
| 4 | Numbers and times mentioned more than once | Note which figure the question actually asks about |
| 5 | Two options from the same word family | Decide the part of speech the gap needs first |
| 6 | A sentence that fits grammatically but not logically | Read the sentences before and after the gap |
| 7 | An option with words copied from the text | Look for the paraphrase, not the identical words |

The Part 7 trap deserves special attention. Correct answers usually restate the passage in different words: a text that says *the shipment will arrive late* is more likely to be matched by *a delivery has been delayed* than by an option repeating *shipment* and *arrive*. Training yourself to spot synonyms is one of the most valuable things you can do for your Reading score.

## A practice order that works

1. **Vocabulary first.** Workplace words appear in every part. Spaced review is the most efficient way to keep them; see [active recall for vocabulary](/en/blog/active-recall-for-vocabulary/).
2. **Parts 1 and 2** to train your ear for short, clear items and build confidence.
3. **Part 5** to fix grammar weak spots: word forms, tenses and articles. Our guides to [a, an, the rules](/en/blog/articles-a-an-the-rules/) and [commonly confused words](/en/blog/commonly-confused-words-in-english/) cover frequent Part 5 traps.
4. **Parts 3, 4, 6 and 7** once the basics are steady, always with a timer.
5. **Full mock tests** every week or two to practice endurance and timing.

For listening more broadly, [how to improve English listening](/en/blog/how-to-improve-english-listening/) explains active listening routines that transfer directly to Parts 3 and 4.

## Quick self-check

1. How many questions are in Part 7?
2. In which part are neither the questions nor the answer options printed?
3. You have 20 minutes left and 30 Part 7 questions unanswered. What should you do?

**Answers:** 1. 54. 2. Part 2 (Part 1 shows only a photo). 3. Work through as many as you can at about one minute each, and fill in a guess for every remaining question before time runs out, since there is no penalty for wrong answers.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions are on the TOEIC, and how long does it take?
The Listening & Reading test has 200 questions and takes about two hours: around 45 minutes for 100 Listening questions and 75 minutes for 100 Reading questions. Allow extra time on the day for check-in.

### Are points deducted for wrong answers?
No. Scores are based on the number of correct answers, so you should answer every question, even if you have to guess.

### Which TOEIC part is the hardest?
Many test takers find Part 7 the most demanding because of its length and time pressure, while others struggle most with Part 3 conversations. Use a diagnostic mock test to find your own weakest part.

### How long is a TOEIC score valid?
ETS does not normally report scores more than two years old, so most employers and schools treat a TOEIC score as valid for two years. Check the requirement of the organization you are applying to.

### What is the maximum TOEIC score?
990 for Listening & Reading combined, with each section worth up to 495 points.
