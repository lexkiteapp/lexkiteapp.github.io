Choosing the wrong IELTS version can cost you a test fee and weeks of waiting. Candidates who book IELTS Academic because it sounds more rigorous can find that Canada's Express Entry cannot use the result, and they have to retake General Training. Conversely, applicants in regulated professions sometimes find that their regulator, not the immigration department, names the version they must take.

Choosing IELTS General vs Academic for immigration comes down to two separate decisions: what your national immigration department permits, and what your occupation's assessing authority stipulates. Both tests evaluate the same four skills and share the same Listening and Speaking modules. Only Reading and Writing differ: General Training uses everyday and workplace texts, Academic uses texts and tasks from university study.

## How IELTS General vs Academic for immigration works in four countries

Immigration authorities set their own boundaries on test acceptance, and they change them. This is what the official pages said on 11 November 2026; recheck the page for your visa on the day you book. For the wider picture of scores and routes, see our [IELTS for immigration guide](/en/blog/ielts-for-immigration-guide/).

### Canada (IRCC)
For economic immigration via Express Entry (Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class, Federal Skilled Trades), Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html)) accepts only **IELTS General Training**, so an Academic result cannot be used and IELTS One Skill Retake is not accepted. Band conversions are in our [IELTS to CLB conversion guide](/en/blog/ielts-clb-score-canada/).

### Australia (Department of Home Affairs)
The [Home Affairs English language page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-support/meeting-our-requirements/english-language) lists both General Training and Academic as accepted for the points tests (subclasses 189, 190 and 491), with each skill judged separately. Your skills assessing authority can ask for more, so read its page for your occupation before booking; the score tiers are in our [IELTS score for Australia PR guide](/en/blog/ielts-score-australia-pr/).

### United Kingdom (UKVI)
For many UK routes, UK Visas and Immigration requires a Secure English Language Test (SELT) such as **IELTS for UKVI**, taken at an approved centre. The [GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) lists the routes. Skilled Worker applicants need CEFR B2, which IELTS for UKVI lists as 5.5 in each component (see ielts.org's page on IELTS for UK visas). That test is booked as IELTS for UKVI and the pages do not split it into Academic or General Training, so you do not choose between the two for those routes. For degree-level study, the student visa accepts IELTS or IELTS for UKVI at 5.5 in each component, and the university may assess your English itself, so ask it before booking. Our [IELTS for UKVI guide](/en/blog/ielts-ukvi-explained/) covers the details.

### New Zealand (Immigration New Zealand)
For skilled residence visas, [Immigration New Zealand](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/process-to-apply/applying-for-a-visa/providing-evidence-and-documents-to-support-your-visa-application/english-language-requirements/english-language-requirements-for-skilled-residence-visas/) accepts either General Training or Academic, and the principal applicant needs an overall 6.5 (details in our [New Zealand residence guide](/en/blog/ielts-new-zealand-skilled-migrant/)).

## The Reading comparison: everyday texts vs dense prose

While the Reading section gives you 60 minutes to answer 40 questions in both formats, the texts themselves differ:

- **General Training Reading:** Divided into three sections. Section 1 contains two or three short factual texts about daily life (bus schedules, hostel notices, community club brochures). Section 2 focuses on workplace context (staff training handbooks, health and safety policies, contractual clauses). Section 3 presents one longer, descriptive prose passage.
- **Academic Reading:** Contains three extensive, complex texts drawn from academic journals, research reports, and analytical books. Topics span paleontology, urban anthropology, behavioural psychology, and materials science.

### The raw score trap
Because General Training Reading materials are more accessible, the band thresholds are stricter. The figures below are approximate, because every test version converts a little differently:
- To achieve **Band 7.0 in General Training Reading**, you must correctly score roughly **34 to 35 out of 40**.
- To achieve **Band 7.0 in Academic Reading**, you typically need **30 out of 40**.

One extra wrong answer can move you from one band to the next, which in a points grid such as Canada's CLB table can cost you valuable points. Practising scanning precision using [skimming and scanning for IELTS Reading](/en/blog/ielts-reading-skimming-and-scanning/) is therefore vital for General Training applicants.

## The Writing comparison: letter writing vs visual data analysis

Writing Task 2 is an essay across both formats (250 words minimum, 40 minutes recommended). However, Academic Task 2 topics lean towards education, science and policy; General Training topics are closer to daily life, work and community.

Task 1 presents the sharpest divergence. If you want to see finished answers, the [IELTS Writing model answers hub](/en/samples/) collects English-language samples:
1. **General Training Task 1 (Letter):** You receive a real-world scenario (complaining to an airline, requesting leave from a supervisor, inviting an old acquaintance) and must produce a letter of at least 150 words within 20 minutes. Success depends on maintaining tone consistency (formal, semi-formal, or personal). Explore our full [IELTS General Training letter guide](/en/blog/ielts-general-training-letter/) for structure and tone.
2. **Academic Task 1 (Report):** You are presented with visual data (pie charts, line graphs, process diagrams, site plans) and must write an objective factual report highlighting major trends, comparisons, and key features without expressing personal conjecture.

## How assessing authorities and universities shift the requirement

If your occupation is regulated, the assessing body or regulator sets its own English rule, and that rule can ask for the Academic version, specific sub-scores or a different test altogether. Doctors, nurses, accountants and engineers should read the current English page of their own regulator (for example the General Medical Council, the Nursing and Midwifery Council, AHPRA or Engineers Australia) before booking. Do not assume the immigration rule is the only one you have to meet.

If you can, get the requirement in writing from the body that will assess you before you schedule your exam date.

## Common candidate misconceptions

1. **"Academic gives bonus immigration points."** False. Immigration departments evaluate verified language ability against their national benchmark grids (e.g., CLB or Australian points tiers). Band 7.5 in GT scores the exact same immigration points as Band 7.5 in Academic.
2. **"General Training is effortless."** GT needs high accuracy in Reading to secure Band 7.0 or 8.0, as the raw score trap above shows.
3. **"I can take IELTS Online at home for PR."** False. Home Affairs, Immigration New Zealand and UKVI do not accept at-home tests, and IRCC does not accept IELTS One Skill Retake. Book an in-person test centre sitting.
4. **"I can mix Reading from one version with Writing from the other."** No. The four skills in one result must come from the same version of IELTS. (One Skill Retake replaces a single skill within the same version and is not accepted for Express Entry.)

**Answers**
1. **IELTS General Training.** Express Entry does not accept Academic for profile calculations.
2. **Academic Reading.** Because texts are denser, roughly 33/40 earns Band 7.5, whereas GT needs about 36/40 (approximate; every test version converts a little differently).
3. **Listening and Speaking.** The audio tracks, questions, and face-to-face speaking interviews are identical.
4. **Register and tone.** Choosing appropriate formal, semi-formal, or informal vocabulary to address the prompt's recipient.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use IELTS Academic for Canada Express Entry if I scored high?

No. The IRCC page says you must take the IELTS General Training option for Express Entry, so an Academic result cannot be used and you would need to retake General Training.

### Are IELTS Listening and Speaking easier in General Training?

No. The Listening and Speaking examinations are completely identical across both formats. Candidates in both test tracks receive the exact same audio files and the same kind of speaking interview.

### How long are IELTS General results valid for immigration?

It depends on the destination. IRCC wants results less than two years old when you complete your profile and when you apply. Immigration New Zealand wants a result no more than two years old when you apply. The UK accepts a SELT for two years from the date it was awarded. Home Affairs accepts results for up to three years from the test date for tests taken from 7 August 2025.
