Many people prepare for months before they notice that the test they practised is not the one their application needs. The question of **IELTS General vs Academic for immigration** has a short practical answer: use the version that your programme names, and prepare for the tasks that are specific to it. This guide explains exactly what is different between the two, why the difference matters for your preparation, and how to avoid the common mistakes.

It is language-test guidance, not legal advice, and no one can guarantee a visa outcome. Always confirm the version on the official page of your own programme; our broader guide to [IELTS for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-for-immigration-guide/) summarises what the Canada, UK, Australia and New Zealand pages said when we checked on 5 November 2026.

## IELTS General vs Academic for immigration: what is the same?

Both versions share the same four skills and the same 0 to 9 band scale. **Listening and Speaking are identical.** In Listening you hear four recordings, and in Speaking you have the same three-part conversation with an examiner. Writing Task 2, the essay, is also the same in both: you give an opinion, discuss views or explain a problem and a solution, in at least 250 words. The marking criteria in Writing and Speaking are the same four for both versions, so a strong essay gets the same credit in either.

That means a large share of your preparation, perhaps half, is shared between the two: listening practice, speaking fluency, essay structure, vocabulary and grammar range.

## What is different: Reading and Writing Task 1

**Reading.** General Training uses texts from everyday life and work: notices, advertisements, timetables, a workplace policy, and a longer descriptive text. Academic uses longer, more complex texts of the kind found in journals, books and magazines, on topics aimed at a general audience but with academic style. Both have 40 questions in 60 minutes, so time pressure is the same, but the vocabulary and the density of ideas differ.

**Writing Task 1.** In General Training it is a **letter**. You respond to a situation: ask for information, complain, apologise, invite, or explain a problem. The recipient may be a friend, a manager or a landlord, so tone matters. In Academic it is a **description of visual information**: a line graph, bar chart, table, pie chart, map or process diagram, in at least 150 words. Different skills are needed: choosing the main features and comparing data in Academic, and writing in the right register in General.

| Task | General Training | Academic |
|---|---|---|
| Task 1 input | A situation in a short prompt | A graph, table, chart, map or diagram |
| Task 1 output | A letter, 150 words or more | A report of features, 150 words or more |
| Key skill | Tone and purpose | Selecting and comparing data |
| Time advice | About 20 minutes | About 20 minutes |

For the letter, see our full guide to the [IELTS General Training letter](/en/blog/ielts-general-training-letter/). For the Academic task, see [IELTS Writing Task 1 line graphs](/en/blog/ielts-writing-task-1-line-graph/).

## Which one does your route ask for?

Do not guess. The rule that protects you is simple: **find the sentence on the official page that names the version, and write it down with the web address and the date.** The pages we reviewed on 5 November 2026 showed these patterns:

- **Canada, Express Entry:** IRCC says IELTS is accepted only as **General Training**. We confirmed this again on 15 November 2026 on the Express Entry language-test page. Academic is not accepted for Express Entry.
- **UK:** if your route needs a Secure English Language Test, you need **IELTS for UKVI**, and the version and level depend on the route. See our guide to [IELTS for UKVI](/en/blog/ielts-ukvi-explained/).
- **Australia:** the Department of Home Affairs sets the required level by skill, and the version may depend on the subclass. Open the page for your subclass before booking.
- **New Zealand:** Immigration New Zealand listed either General or Academic for skilled residence on the page we read, but check the current rules.

Universities, professional bodies and registration boards often ask for Academic, and they list their own rules. If you are applying for study as well as migration, you may need both pages open.

## What to do if you choose wrongly

If you have already sat the wrong version, do not assume it is useless. Some systems accept both. Check the current rules for your programme, and if only the other version is accepted, plan a new test. Remember that validity rules may mean the old result expires, so counting dates carefully helps. A mistake in the version is a booking and planning problem, not a reflection of your English. Your English skills still count towards the next attempt.

## How to prepare for the version you need

Because Listening, Speaking and essay writing are shared, the plan is easy to split:

1. **Shared core (about half your time).** Daily listening practice, speaking aloud, vocabulary and grammar, and Task 2 essays. See our guide to [IELTS Writing Task 2 structure](/en/blog/ielts-writing-task-2-structure/) and [IELTS Speaking Part 2](/en/blog/ielts-speaking-part-2-tips/).
2. **Version-specific work.** For General Training, write one letter per tone each week and practise scanning notices and advertisements. For Academic, practise describing different chart types and reading long texts under time pressure.
3. **Timed practice.** In the last four weeks, do full timed tests in your version, because pacing is part of the skill.

If you are not sure how long you need, use your target scores to build a plan. Our [IELTS self-study plan](/en/blog/ielts-self-study-plan/) can be adjusted for either version.

## Is General Training easier?

It is not automatically easier. Texts are more everyday, and the letter task asks for less specialised data analysis. But the scale and the marking are the same, and you still need accurate grammar, a good range of vocabulary and clear organisation to reach a high band. Candidates who assume it is easy often under-prepare Writing, where tone, a clear purpose and full coverage of the bullet points decide the mark. Choose the version by the requirement, not by perceived difficulty.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the band score scale the same for General and Academic?

Yes. Both use the 0 to 9 band scale and the same descriptors for Writing and Speaking. The test content is different in Reading and Writing Task 1.

### Can I switch from General Training to Academic after registering?

Rules depend on the test provider and the centre, and fees may apply. Check with the centre before you register, rather than relying on a later change.

### Which version do most immigration programmes need?

It varies by country and programme. Canada's Express Entry names General Training, while other systems may accept either or name a specific version. Always read your own page.

### Does one version have a higher band ceiling?

No. Both can reach band 9. The difference is the type of text and the Task 1 genre.
