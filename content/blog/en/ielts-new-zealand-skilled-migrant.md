New Zealand's immigration policy sets distinct expectations for language competence. The IELTS score for New Zealand residence is the same 6.5 overall for the principal applicant across the Skilled Migrant Category and the other skilled residence visas, and it must be evidenced with your application.

According to Immigration New Zealand ([immigration.govt.nz](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/process-to-apply/applying-for-a-visa/providing-evidence-and-documents-to-support-your-visa-application/english-language-requirements/english-language-requirements-for-skilled-residence-visas/)), the English requirement applies to the principal applicant and to partners and children aged 16 and over. Check that page on the day you apply, because Immigration New Zealand changes its instructions from time to time (this article was checked on 11 November 2026). Understanding minimum bands, partner rules, test validity and retake policy protects you from costly delays.

## The IELTS score for New Zealand residence that principal applicants need

For principal applicants under the Skilled Migrant Category and the other skilled residence visas, the baseline language benchmark is clear:

{{diagram: d1 | Immigration New Zealand English language requirements for skilled residence.}}

### The 6.5 overall band requirement
- **Minimum score:** An **overall band score of 6.5 or higher**.
- **Accepted formats:** Immigration New Zealand accepts either **IELTS General Training** or **IELTS Academic**. Unlike some jurisdictions that restrict economic migrants strictly to General Training, New Zealand allows applicants to submit Academic scores if they previously completed the test for university admission or professional licensing.
- **One Skill Retake (OSR):** Immigration New Zealand officially recognises IELTS One Skill Retake, enabling candidates who achieved strong marks across three skills but fell short in one component to retake that single paper without repeating the entire four-skill exam.

To evaluate which format best aligns with your strengths, read our detailed comparison of [IELTS General vs Academic for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-general-vs-academic-immigration/).

## Requirements for secondary applicants: partners and dependent children

If you include your partner or dependent children aged 16 and over in your residence application, they must also demonstrate English capability:

- **The standard threshold:** Secondary applicants must achieve an **overall band score of at least 5.0** in either IELTS General Training or Academic.
- **Exemptions:** the test can be skipped in these cases, as stated on the page (check it, because it changes):
  - Citizens of Canada, Ireland, the UK or the US who have worked or studied for at least five years in one of those countries, Australia or New Zealand.
  - People with a qualification comparable to a New Zealand level 7 bachelor's degree, gained in a listed country such as Australia or New Zealand, who lived there for two years while studying.
  - People with a level 8 or higher qualification of that kind who lived there for one year while studying.
- **English lessons option:** If a partner or adult dependent does not reach the minimum, you can choose English lessons in New Zealand when you apply. You pay only if the visa is approved, and a discount depends on the test scores. This option is not available to the principal applicant.

## Test validity and physical centre rules under New Zealand instructions

Immigration regulations impose two strict operational constraints on test submissions:

1. **The 2-year validity window:** Your test result must be **no more than 2 years old** when you apply. Check its age on the day you apply; an older result cannot be used.
2. **Physical test centre mandatory:** Tests delivered completely online from home—such as IELTS Online—are strictly inadmissible for New Zealand residence applications. You must attend an approved, audited brick-and-mortar test venue in person.

Comparing international frameworks highlights how rules vary across the Pacific; for instance, Australia allows up to three years of validity for skilled points for tests taken from 7 August 2025, as outlined in our [IELTS score for Australia PR guide](/en/blog/ielts-score-australia-pr/). Similarly, UK visa applicants must adhere to distinct SELT audit protocols described in our guide on [IELTS for UKVI explained](/en/blog/ielts-ukvi-explained/).

## Tactical preparation to secure Band 6.5+ across all papers

Achieving Band 6.5 overall requires balanced competence across receptive and productive skills:

1. **Targeting high raw scores in Reading and Listening:** In General Training Reading, Band 6.5 needs roughly 32 to 33 correct answers out of 40 (approximate; every test version converts a little differently). Efficient text navigation prevents time pressure during the third, denser passage.
2. **Structuring Task 2 essays:** Writing is often the skill to watch if your practice scores sit around Band 6.0. Master paragraph progression, clear topic sentences, and balanced counter-arguments with our [IELTS Writing Task 2 structure tutorial](/en/blog/ielts-writing-task-2-structure/) and the [IELTS Writing model answers hub](/en/samples/).
3. **Developing spoken spontaneity:** Aim for Speaking answers that you can extend for the full Part 2 talk without long pauses. Practise topic development using our [IELTS Speaking Part 2 tips](/en/blog/ielts-speaking-part-2-tips/).
4. **Planning around your partner:** their minimum is 5.0 overall, lower than yours, so decide early whether they will sit the test or choose the English lessons option, and book dates that do not clash with yours.
5. **Keeping the two-year window in mind:** a result that is fine today may be too old if your application is delayed, so work backwards from your planned application date when you book.

## Common mistakes in New Zealand residence applications

1. **Leaving the test until the last minute:** you need the result when you apply, so book early enough that results arrive before your deadline (check the instructions for your visa).
2. **Neglecting partner language evidence:** Assuming a spouse is automatically exempt without verifying passport status or academic credentials causes processing delays.
3. **Sitting IELTS Online:** Booking the remote home test edition renders the certificate invalid for residence lodgement.
4. **Overlooking One Skill Retake time limits:** If you plan to use OSR to lift a sub-score, check the IELTS OSR rules for the deadline before you book.
5. **Assuming that years of work in an English-speaking office exempt you:** exemptions depend on citizenship plus years of work or study, or on a qualification.

## Other skilled residence visas

The same Immigration New Zealand English page covers Skilled Migrant Category Resident, Work to Residence, Straight to Residence (the Green List route), Care Workforce Work to Residence and Transport Work to Residence, so the 6.5 overall principal-applicant score and the 5.0 partner score in the table apply across them. Confirm which visa you are applying for before you book.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I submit IELTS Academic for New Zealand skilled residence?

Yes. Immigration New Zealand accepts both IELTS Academic and IELTS General Training for principal applicants and accompanying family members.

### What happens if my partner scores Band 4.5 instead of 5.0?

If your partner does not meet the 5.0 overall band threshold and does not qualify for an exemption, you can choose English lessons in New Zealand when you apply and pay only if the visa is approved.

### Does New Zealand grant exemptions based on work experience?

Not on its own. Work experience alone is not an exemption. Exemptions depend on citizenship plus five years of work or study in certain countries, or on a qualification (see the exemptions above), and otherwise you need a test result.
