If you are searching for the **IELTS score for New Zealand residence**, the rule is fairly simple. For the Skilled Migrant Category, Immigration New Zealand asks the principal applicant for **IELTS 6.5 overall**, and it does not set a minimum for each skill when you use IELTS. Partners and children aged 16 and over need a lower overall band. This article reads the official page, explains what it means for planning, and shows who may not need a test at all.

## The IELTS score for New Zealand residence: what the page says

We read the Immigration New Zealand page on English language requirements for skilled residence visas on **17 November 2026**. It covers the Skilled Migrant Category Resident visa; the same page also serves a few other skilled residence visas, so check yours. For IELTS it says:

| | Principal applicant | Partner or dependent child aged 16+ |
|---|---|---|
| IELTS (General or Academic) | 6.5 or more overall | 5.0 or more overall |

*Source: Immigration New Zealand, English language requirements for skilled residence visas (immigration.govt.nz). Checked 17 November 2026.*

{{diagram: d1 | The IELTS overall band for each type of applicant: 6.5 for the principal applicant and 5.0 for a partner or child aged 16 and over, with no skill minimum listed for IELTS}}

Three things matter here.

**It is an overall band.** For IELTS the page gives one figure, and it lists no minimum for Listening, Reading, Writing or Speaking. That is different from some other countries, where each skill must reach a band. If you want to see how the overall band is calculated, read [IELTS band scores explained](/en/blog/ielts-band-scores-explained/).

**General Training and Academic are both accepted.** The page lists "General or Academic". So the version you take is a practical choice: pick the one you can prepare for best. Our article on [IELTS General vs Academic for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-general-vs-academic-immigration/) compares them.

**Other tests have their own figures.** The same page also approves TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, Cambridge B2 First and OET, and gives scores for all of them except OET. This article does not reproduce them. If you are comparing tests, open the page and read the rows for the test you are considering.

## Rules around the score: validity and format

The number is only half of the rule. The English page and the Skilled Migrant Category visa page add conditions that cause real problems when ignored.

- **Two years.** Results must be no more than 2 years old at the time you apply. If you are waiting for an offer or a job check, count from your test date, not from the day you start planning.
- **In person.** The test must be taken at a test centre. At-home or remote tests are not acceptable.
- **No future booking.** The visa page says you cannot provide evidence of a future booking. A result must already exist.
- **A longer window in one case.** If the principal applicant or a family member holds an occupational registration that earns points on the points-based pathway, results can be up to 5 years old. That is stated on the visa page, not the English page, so read both.

IELTS One Skill Retake is listed as accepted on the English page. That can help if a single skill is the problem, but check the details on the page before you plan around it.

## Who may not need a test, and other routes

A test is not the only way to show English. The page lists exemptions. In short:

- **Citizens** of Canada, Ireland, the United Kingdom or the United States who have spent at least 5 years working or studying in one of those countries, Australia or New Zealand.
- **Degree holders.** A bachelor-level qualification comparable to NZ level 7, gained in Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, the United Kingdom or the United States, with at least 2 years of living there while studying. A postgraduate-level qualification (NZ level 8 or above) from the same countries needs at least 1 year of living there.
- **Children under 16** have no English requirement. Partners and children aged 16 and over can also meet it by paying for English lessons in New Zealand. Principal applicants cannot use the lessons option.

Exact conditions are on the page, and it asks for evidence such as passport details or a certificate and transcript. Read the exact wording on the page before deciding you do not need to test.

## How to plan for a 6.5 overall

Because only the overall band counts, the risk is a profile with one very low skill. Overall is the average of the four skills rounded to the nearest half band, so a 5.5 in Writing can be offset by strong scores elsewhere. That makes the planning question practical: where can you gain the most for the least effort?

Here is a made-up example. A candidate gets Listening 7.0, Reading 6.5, Writing 5.5 and Speaking 6.0. The average is 6.25, which IELTS rounds to a 6.5 overall. The page asks for 6.5 overall, so the figure meets the requirement. In practice, though, a result like this has no margin: one half-band drop in Listening and the overall falls to 6.0. A sensible plan lifts the weakest skill and builds a margin, rather than aiming at exactly 6.5.

For each skill, the usual first fixes are:

- **Writing:** answer every part of the task and organise the text. Our [IELTS Writing mistakes](/en/blog/ielts-writing-mistakes/) list shows the most common losses.
- **Speaking:** extend answers beyond one sentence; see [IELTS Speaking Part 3 strategies for extending answers](/en/blog/ielts-speaking-part-3-strategies-for-extending-answers/).
- **Listening and Reading:** time and accuracy; [IELTS Listening tips](/en/blog/ielts-listening-tips/) and [skimming and scanning for Reading](/en/blog/ielts-reading-skimming-and-scanning/) cover both.

## Before you book: a short checklist

- Read the English page for skilled residence visas and your visa page on the same day.
- Decide whether an exemption applies to you and to your partner.
- Confirm the test is in person and the result will be no more than 2 years old when you apply.
- Check whether you are the principal applicant or a partner, because the bands differ.
- Save your Test Report Form so it is ready for the application.

## Frequently asked questions

### What IELTS score do I need for New Zealand residence?

For the Skilled Migrant Category, the page lists 6.5 or more overall for the principal applicant and 5.0 or more overall for a partner or dependent child aged 16 and over. It lists no per-skill minimum for IELTS.

### Do Academic and General Training both count?

Yes. The page accepts the General or Academic module of IELTS.

### How long is my IELTS result valid?

Results must be no more than 2 years old at the time you apply, with one exception tied to occupational registration that the visa page describes.

### Can I take IELTS at home?

No. The page says at-home or remote tests are not acceptable; the test must be taken at a test centre.

### Can I skip the test?

Some people can, for example certain citizens with years of work or study in listed countries, or degree holders who lived in listed countries while studying. The page sets conditions and evidence, so read it before you decide.

### Can I predict my official band from practice results?

Not reliably. Mock scores from any source, ours included, are a rough guide to progress. Only the real exam issues an official band, so treat practice numbers as a hint about what to revise, never as a forecast.
