**TOEIC Part 6** is the text completion part of the Reading section. You read four short texts, such as emails, notices and advertisements, each with four gaps, so there are 16 questions in total. Most gaps test grammar or vocabulary in context, and one gap in each text asks you to choose a whole sentence that fits. Part 6 looks like Part 5, but it rewards candidates who read beyond the sentence with the gap. This guide explains the three kinds of item, gives a method, and uses original examples.

## Where TOEIC Part 6 sits in the test

The Reading section has three parts: Part 5 (incomplete sentences, 30 questions), Part 6 (text completion, 16 questions) and Part 7 (reading comprehension, 54 questions). Together they fill 75 minutes. Part 6 is the bridge between the two: you use the grammar and vocabulary of Part 5 inside connected text. The full layout is in the [TOEIC test format guide](/en/blog/toeic-test-format/), and the sentence-level skills come from [TOEIC Part 5 tips](/en/blog/toeic-part-5-tips/) and [Part 5 grammar and vocabulary strategies](/en/blog/toeic-part-5-grammar-and-vocabulary-strategies/).

## The three kinds of gap

{{diagram: d1 | Know which kind of gap you are looking at before you choose an answer}}

**Grammar gaps** ask for the correct tense, word form, pronoun or preposition. Sometimes the answer depends on information outside the sentence, such as a date in the previous sentence that decides between past and present perfect.

**Vocabulary gaps** offer four words of similar meaning or the same family. The correct answer must fit the meaning and the collocation: *submit an application* but *file a complaint*.

**Sentence insertion gaps** ask you to choose the sentence that belongs in the gap. Linking words, pronouns and topics decide the answer.

## A routine for each text

{{diagram: d2 | Skim first, read around each gap, and insert the full sentence last}}

1. **Skim the text for type and purpose.** Is it an email to staff, a notice to customers, an announcement? This tells you the register and tenses.
2. **Read the sentence before and after each gap.** Do not read each gap in isolation.
3. **Decide the kind of gap and the clue.**
4. **Choose the form that fits the whole context.**
5. **Do the sentence-insertion item last.** After you have understood the paragraph, the missing sentence is easier to find.
6. **Read the finished text once.** Does it make sense from beginning to end?

A common plan is about 40 to 60 seconds per gap, so roughly 10 to 14 minutes for all four texts; if Part 5 is quick for you, use the time you save here. See the timing advice in [TOEIC Part 7 strategies](/en/blog/toeic-part-7-reading-comprehension-strategies/).

## Original example: a vocabulary and grammar gap

> *To: All staff. Subject: Office renovation. Please note that the third floor will be closed from Monday for repairs. Employees who normally work there ----(1)---- relocated to the second floor. We apologise for any inconvenience. The work ----(2)---- by the end of the month.*

(1) options: *will be, were, had been, have*. The email talks about a future closure, so *will be relocated* is correct, and the past forms cannot fit.

(2) options: *completes, will complete, has been completing, will be completed*. The work is the subject and the action is done to it, and the notice looks to the future, so *will be completed* is correct.

A grammar item like (1) needs the time reference in the sentence before; the closure begins *from Monday*, so past tenses cannot be right.

## Original example: a sentence-insertion gap

> *Thank you for contacting our customer service team. We have received your request for a replacement part. ----(3)---- It should arrive within five working days. If you do not receive it, please reply to this email.*

Options:
- A. Our offices are closed on public holidays.
- B. The new part was sent to your address this morning.
- C. Many customers choose to pay by credit card.
- D. We are looking for new employees.

The answer is B. The next sentence uses *it* to refer to a part that was sent; only B introduces something *it* could refer to. A, C and D are unrelated to the request.

When you choose a sentence, test these four things: does it match the topic, does the pronoun have something to refer to, does the time fit, and does the next sentence follow naturally?

## Linking words and pronouns

Text completion often depends on cohesion. Connect the gap to the sentences around it:

| Clue | Example |
|---|---|
| Contrast | *however, although, on the other hand* means the idea changes |
| Addition | *in addition, also, moreover* means the idea continues |
| Result | *therefore, as a result* means the previous sentence is a cause |
| Reference | *this, these, it, they* need a clear noun before them |
| Time | *then, afterwards, by next week* need a logical order |

If a gap includes a connector, the correct option is often decided by the sentence before. For instance, *We tried to contact you by phone. ----, we have sent you an email* needs a connector such as *Therefore* or *As a result*, not *However*.

## What the clues look like in practice

Most Part 6 errors come from skipping a clue that was there. Three habits help. First, circle every date, deadline or time word in the text, because they decide tense. Second, underline the noun that follows the gap, because it often shows whether you need an adjective, an adverb or a verb form. Third, notice repeated nouns: if a text speaks about *the shipment* in the first paragraph and *it* in the second, the sentence you insert must keep that thread.

Keep a short list of the clue types that catch you out. After two weeks you will see that the same three or four patterns cause most of your errors, and fixing those few is faster than studying new rules.

## Common mistakes

1. **Reading each sentence alone.** Part 6 clues often sit in the previous sentence.
2. **Choosing a word that is right in general but wrong in this text.** *Submit* and *file* are both verbs, but each fits different nouns.
3. **Doing the sentence-insertion item first.** Understand the paragraph first.
4. **Ignoring the type of text.** A formal notice and a friendly email use different language.
5. **Spending too long.** If a gap takes more than a minute, guess and return later.

## A two-week practice plan

| Days | Practice |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Do one text a day; write down the clue for each answer |
| 4–6 | Focus on sentence insertion: underline pronouns and connectors |
| 7–9 | Focus on collocations and verb forms from your error log |
| 10–12 | Do two texts under time pressure |
| 13–14 | Do a full Part 5 and 6 set in about 25 minutes |

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions are in TOEIC Part 6?

There are 16 questions: four texts with four gaps each. In each text, one gap asks you to choose a sentence.

### How is Part 6 different from Part 5?

Part 5 tests single sentences, while Part 6 tests gaps inside connected text, so context and cohesion matter more.

### Should I read the whole text before answering?

Skim it first to understand the type and purpose, then read the sentences around each gap carefully. You do not need to read every word twice.

### How much time should I spend on Part 6?

Aim for roughly 10 to 14 minutes for all four texts, but adjust to your own pace. Take time from Part 6 only if you are sure of the answers.
