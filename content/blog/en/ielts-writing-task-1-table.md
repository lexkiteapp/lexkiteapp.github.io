An **IELTS Writing Task 1 table** looks frightening because it holds so many numbers, but the task is not to report them all. You have about 20 minutes and at least 150 words, and the examiner wants to see that you can select the most important figures, group them logically and compare them accurately. Pie charts ask for the same skill with percentages that add up to a whole. This guide gives a method for both, an original table with a model report, and the language you need for comparisons.

## A method for an IELTS Writing Task 1 table

{{diagram: d2 | Select first, then group, then write}}

1. **Read the title, units and categories (1 minute).** Is it hours, percentages, thousands, dollars? Which years or groups?
2. **Find extremes (1 minute).** Mark the highest and lowest value in each row and column.
3. **Find a pattern (1 minute).** Does one group always lead? Does one category change most?
4. **Group (1 minute).** Decide on two groups, for example the three highest figures and the rest, or younger versus older groups.
5. **Write (14 minutes) and check (2 minutes).**

## An original table

*The table shows the average number of hours per week that people in three age groups spent on four leisure activities in 2023. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features.*

| Activity | Ages 15–24 | Ages 25–54 | Ages 55+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Watching television | 8 | 12 | 20 |
| Using social media | 14 | 7 | 3 |
| Sport and exercise | 6 | 4 | 3 |
| Reading | 2 | 3 | 8 |

## A model report

> The table compares the weekly hours that three age groups devoted to four leisure activities in 2023.
>
> Overall, television and social media took up the most time, and the two activities showed opposite patterns by age: the youngest group favoured social media, whereas the oldest group watched the most television.
>
> Young people aged 15 to 24 spent 14 hours a week on social media, which was far higher than the figures for the 25 to 54 group (7 hours) and the over-55s (3 hours). They also did the most sport, at 6 hours, compared with 4 and 3 hours respectively. By contrast, they read for only 2 hours.
>
> Television was the most popular activity among older people. Those aged 55 and over watched for 20 hours, which was more than double the figure for the youngest group (8 hours) and well above that of the middle group (12 hours). Reading also increased with age, reaching 8 hours for the oldest group, four times the level of the youngest.

The report has about 170 words. It contains no explanations for the differences because the table does not give any.

## Checks before you write the first sentence

Spend one more minute on three questions. First, are the numbers totals, averages or percentages? A table of averages cannot tell you how many people did something, so do not say *most people* unless the data supports it. Second, are any groups missing or combined? If the table has no data for one group, do not guess. Third, is there a footnote or a different unit in one column? Examiners look for exactly these details, and a misread unit changes every sentence you write afterwards.

## What the report does well

- The **overview** states the two clearest patterns without detailed numbers.
- The **grouping** is by age: the second paragraph centres on the youngest group, the third on the oldest, and the middle group appears as a comparison in both.
- The **figures are accurate**: 20 is more than double 8; 8 is four times 2.
- The **language varies**: *devoted to*, *took up*, *favoured*, *well above*, *respectively*, *by contrast*.
- It leaves out one figure (the middle group's reading time) and groups the rest by age.

## Pie charts: the same skill, different data

A pie chart shows shares of one whole. The overview should name the largest and smallest shares, and mention any clear majority or near-equal sections. Describe percentages and fractions accurately:

| Value | Ways to say it |
|---|---|
| 25% | a quarter, one in four |
| 50% | a half, half of |
| 75% | three quarters |
| 10% | one tenth, one in ten |
| 33% | a third, around a third |
| Just over 50% | a small majority |

Original example: *Spending on housing made up 40 per cent of the household budget, almost twice the share spent on food (22 per cent), while transport accounted for one eighth.* Notice that the report uses a mix of percentages and fractions and does not repeat the same pattern.

If two pie charts compare years, report the biggest change in share and one stable category. Take care to say *percentage points* for differences between shares and *per cent* for the shares themselves.

## Language for comparing figures

| Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|
| Higher | *higher than, exceeded, well above, twice as high as* |
| Lower | *lower than, far below, only a fraction of* |
| Similar | *similar to, roughly equal, almost identical* |
| Contrast | *whereas, while, by contrast, in contrast* |
| Approximation | *about, approximately, just over, nearly, around* |
| Ranking | *the highest, the second highest, the lowest* |

Use *respectively* only when the order of the figures is clear, and avoid starting every sentence with the same pattern. For more variety, practise [complex sentences for IELTS writing](/en/blog/complex-sentences-for-ielts-writing/) and the connectors in [linking words for IELTS writing](/en/blog/linking-words-for-ielts-writing/).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Reporting every number | Select extremes and patterns |
| No overview | Add one paragraph with the two main features |
| Mixing units | Check whether the figures are hours, per cent or thousands |
| Explaining reasons | Describe only what the data shows |
| Wrong comparison language | Check that *more than double* is really true |
| Inconsistent tense | Past data takes past tenses; a pie chart without a date takes present simple |

## Frequently asked questions

### How many numbers should I include?

Include the figures that support your overview and the most important comparisons. For a table, that is often eight to twelve numbers in total, not all of them.

### Is a pie chart harder than a table?

Not usually. Pie charts have fewer numbers but require accurate use of percentages and fractions.

### Do I need to write a conclusion in Task 1?

No separate conclusion is required, because the overview serves that purpose. A short final sentence is optional.

### What if two categories have the same value?

Say so directly: *the two were identical at 12 per cent.* Do not invent a difference.
