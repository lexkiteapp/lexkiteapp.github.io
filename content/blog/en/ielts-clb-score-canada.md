Searching for an **IELTS to CLB conversion** usually means one thing: you have a target Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) level, and you need to know which IELTS bands to aim for. The answer is not one number. Canada converts your result **skill by skill**, so Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking each get their own CLB level. Below is the official table from the Government of Canada, what it means in practice, and how to turn it into a study plan.

## What CLB is and why IELTS scores get converted

CLB stands for Canadian Language Benchmarks, a scale of language ability used across Canadian immigration programs. Programs set their language requirement as a CLB level, not as an IELTS band. If you take IELTS, your four skill scores are converted to CLB levels using an equivalency chart published by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).

That chart is the source of truth. Blog posts, agency tables and forum threads copy it, and copies can be out of date. This article reproduces the figures as we checked them on **5 November 2026** on IRCC's [Express Entry language test page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) (page dated 16 October 2026). The same IELTS rows appear on other IRCC program pages, such as the Agri-Food Pilot and post-graduation work permit pages. IRCC can change its charts, so check the live page before you book a test.

For the bigger picture (which test, which country, how long results stay valid), start with our guide to [IELTS for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-for-immigration-guide/). This spoke article is only about the Canadian conversion.

## The official IELTS to CLB conversion table (General Training)

The chart lists the **IELTS General Training** test. Make sure the version you book matches what your program accepts, and read the program page before you pay for a test.

| CLB level | Listening | Reading | Writing | Speaking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 and above | 8.5 | 8.0 | 7.5 | 7.5 |
| 9 | 8.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 |
| 8 | 7.5 | 6.5 | 6.5 | 6.5 |
| 7 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 |
| 6 | 5.5 | 5.0 | 5.5 | 5.5 |
| 5 | 5.0 | 4.0 | 5.0 | 5.0 |
| 4 | 4.5 | 3.5 | 4.0 | 4.0 |

*Source: IRCC, Express Entry language test results page, IELTS General Training table (canada.ca). Checked 5 November 2026. IRCC lists CLB 10 as a range, for example 8.5 to 9.0 in Listening.*

Read each row as "the IELTS band you need in that skill to be assigned that CLB level". Three patterns are worth noticing.

**Listening is the steep one at the top.** CLB 9 needs 8.0 and CLB 10 needs 8.5, while Reading, Writing and Speaking need 7.0 and 7.5 to 8.0 at the same two levels. If you want a high CLB level, Listening is where many candidates have to push hardest.

**CLB 7 is a flat 6.0.** Every skill converts at 6.0, which makes the first serious target easy to remember. Many programs use CLB 7 as a threshold, but the required level depends on the program and sometimes on the skill, so check yours.

**The scale is not evenly spaced.** From CLB 7 to CLB 8, Reading, Writing and Speaking rise by just half a band, while Listening jumps by 1.5. Half a band can be the difference between two levels.

## How to read your own IELTS result against the chart

Your IELTS report shows four skill bands and an overall band. For CLB, **the overall band is not used**. Use this routine:

1. Write down your four skill bands.
2. For each skill, find the highest row whose number you meet or beat.
3. That row's CLB level is your level in that skill.
4. Compare each skill with the level your program asks for.

Here is a made-up example. Suppose a candidate gets Listening 8.0, Reading 6.5, Writing 6.0 and Speaking 7.0. Following the table, that is CLB 9 in Listening, CLB 8 in Reading, CLB 7 in Writing and CLB 9 in Speaking. The overall band looks comfortable, yet Writing is the skill holding the profile back if the program wants CLB 8 or more in every skill.

What this means for planning is simple: **your weakest skill, not your average, decides what you can show.** A candidate who needs CLB 7 across the board has a clear task: reach 6.0 in all four skills in one sitting. A strong Speaking score does not rescue a 5.0 in Reading.

The table also does not say how a CLB level counts toward any selection score or ranking. That is set by each program, so use its official page for that, not a blog article.

## What CLB 7 and CLB 9 feel like as IELTS targets

A band on paper means little until you know what it takes to hit it in a real test. These are practical observations from teaching, not official descriptions.

**Aiming for 6.0 in every skill (CLB 7)** is mostly about removing weak spots. Candidates often arrive with a good skill and a bad one. Typical blockers are running out of time in Reading, losing the thread in Listening Section 3 and 4, a Writing answer that does not fully address the task, and Speaking answers that stop after one sentence. Fix the weakest skill first. The article on [how to break an IELTS band plateau](/en/blog/how-to-break-ielts-band-plateau/) helps if you keep landing at 5.5.

**Aiming for 7.0 and 8.0 (CLB 9)** is a different kind of work. Accuracy matters more than volume: fewer slips in grammar, more precise vocabulary, and answers that develop ideas instead of listing them. For Listening at 8.0, you cannot afford to lose points on small things such as plurals, spelling and number formats. [IELTS Listening tips](/en/blog/ielts-listening-tips/) covers those habits.

Between the two, a useful rule is to practise the sub-skills that decide the score in that band. For Reading, that is speed and precise matching, see [skimming and scanning for IELTS Reading](/en/blog/ielts-reading-skimming-and-scanning/). For Writing, it is task response and structure, see [IELTS Writing Task 2 structure](/en/blog/ielts-writing-task-2-structure/) and the [General Training letter guide](/en/blog/ielts-general-training-letter/). For Speaking, it is extending answers, see [IELTS Speaking Part 2 tips](/en/blog/ielts-speaking-part-2-tips/).

## Other tests and the same idea

IELTS is not the only test IRCC accepts. For Express Entry, the same page lists CELPIP-General and PTE Core for English, and TEF Canada and TCF Canada for French. It also says IELTS One Skill Retake is not accepted for Express Entry, so a weak skill means a full retest. Each test is converted skill by skill using its own table. We have not reproduced those scores here; if you are comparing tests, open the IRCC page and read the rows for the test you are considering.

## Build a study plan from the chart

Once you know the bands, work backwards.

1. **Find the CLB level your program asks for**, on the official page of that program.
2. **Look up the minimum IELTS band in each skill** using the table above.
3. **Measure where you are.** A short level check shows which skills are close and which are far. You can [take the 5-minute placement test](/app/#/placement) in Lexkite for a rough picture. It is a study tool and not a prediction of an official band.
4. **Pick the gap that is largest** and give it most of your weekly time.
5. **Set an exam date and weekly hours**, then work backwards.

4. Why does the overall band not help you read this chart?

**Answers.**

1. 6.5.
2. Listening, 8.5.
3. Listening is CLB 7. Reading, Writing and Speaking are CLB 9. The Listening score is the only one holding the candidate back.
4. Because each skill is converted separately; one weak skill cannot be balanced by a strong one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which IELTS test do I take for Canadian immigration?

IELTS General Training. The Express Entry page says IELTS must be the General Training test, and the chart above is for that version only. Other programs can have their own rules, so confirm on the program page before booking.

### How long are IELTS results valid for Express Entry?

IRCC says results must be less than two years old both when you create your Express Entry profile and when you submit your permanent residence application. If your result could expire while you wait for an invitation, plan a retest date early.

### Is IELTS 6.0 in every skill equal to CLB 7?

Yes, in the IRCC chart reproduced above, 6.0 in Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking converts to CLB 7. If any skill falls below 6.0, that skill falls below CLB 7.

### Does my overall band count?

For this conversion, no. The chart works skill by skill. The overall band is a summary on your report, not an input to the table.

### Can I predict my official band from practice results?

No. Practice tools, including ours, can show progress and weak areas, but only the real test produces an official band. Use practice scores as a guide for what to study next, not as a promise.
