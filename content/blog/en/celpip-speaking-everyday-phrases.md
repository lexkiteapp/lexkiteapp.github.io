Many people prepare for the CELPIP speaking test by memorising full answers. That usually backfires: the tasks change, and a speech that does not match the question sounds strange. A better plan is to learn a set of flexible **CELPIP speaking phrases**, short everyday chunks you can slot into any answer. This guide shows the phrases for each task type, how to link ideas smoothly, and how to keep going when you lose a word.

CELPIP is an English test used mostly for Canadian immigration and citizenship. The speaking part is done on a computer, with short preparation time and a time-limited recorded answer for each of the eight tasks. Check the official CELPIP website for current timings and scoring details, because they can change.

## The eight tasks in one view

| Task | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1. Giving advice | Advise someone in a given situation |
| 2. Talking about a personal experience | Tell a short story from your life |
| 3. Describing a scene | Describe what you see in a picture |
| 4. Making predictions | Say what might happen next in the scene |
| 5. Comparing and persuading | Choose one option and explain why |
| 6. Dealing with a difficult situation | Handle a problem, often with a polite request or complaint |
| 7. Expressing opinions | Give and support your view |
| 8. Describing an unusual situation | Describe something strange and explain it |

The tasks are different, but the language needed is not. In each, you must answer the question quickly, give reasons or details, and sound like a real person in everyday Canadian life.

## CELPIP speaking phrases by task

### Task 1: Giving advice

- *"If I were you, I would…"*
- *"I think the best option is to…"*
- *"You might want to think about…"*
- *"Another thing worth considering is…"*
- *"That way, you can avoid…"*

Example: *"If I were you, I would book the earlier flight. That way, you can avoid rushing in the morning, and you will have time to relax."*

### Task 2: Talking about a personal experience

- *"One memorable time was when…"*
- *"It all started when…"*
- *"At first I was nervous, but…"*
- *"Looking back, I learned that…"*

Use the **past simple** for the story and keep details concrete: where, when, who and how you felt. Our guide to [present perfect vs past simple](/en/blog/present-perfect-vs-past-simple/) helps you choose the right tense.

### Tasks 3 and 4: Describing a scene and making predictions

- *"In the foreground, there is…"* / *"In the background, I can see…"*
- *"On the left, a woman is…"* / *"It looks like…"* / *"It seems that…"*
- *"The people seem to be…"*
- *"I think it is likely that…"* / *"Probably, they will…"* / *"They might end up…"*

Describe, do not guess wildly. Say what you see, then add what it suggests. For predictions, use *will*, *might* and *is likely to*, and connect each prediction to something visible: *"Because the sky is dark, they will probably go inside soon."*

### Task 5: Comparing and persuading

- *"Both options are good, but I would choose…"*
- *"The main reason is…"*
- *"Compared with the other one, this one is…"*
- *"I know the other option has some advantages, but…"*

Pick quickly. There is no right choice; your reasons matter.

### Task 6: Dealing with a difficult situation

- *"I am calling because…"* / *"I am sorry to bother you, but…"*
- *"Would it be possible to…?"* / *"I was wondering if you could…"*
- *"I would really appreciate it if…"*
- *"I understand this might be inconvenient, but…"*

Keep a polite, calm tone, even when the scenario is a complaint. Our guide to [polite English at work](/en/blog/polite-english-at-work/) has more softening phrases.

### Task 7: Expressing opinions

- *"Personally, I believe that…"* / *"In my opinion,…"*
- *"The main reason I feel this way is…"*
- *"For example, last year I…"*
- *"Some people disagree, but I think…"*

### Task 8: Describing an unusual situation

- *"What is strange about this is…"*
- *"It reminds me of…"*
- *"I have never seen anything like this before."*
- *"One possible explanation is…"*

## A model answer you can adapt

Task: *A friend is choosing between a quiet apartment far from work and a noisy one close to work. Give advice.*

*"If I were you, I would take the apartment close to work. The main reason is time: you will save about an hour every day, and that adds up. Also, the noise is only a problem at night, and you can fix that with earplugs or a better window. On top of that, living near work makes it easy to join evening activities. So overall, I think the closer apartment is the better choice."*

Notice what happens: the answer comes in the first sentence, there are two reasons with detail, and the last line is short. Nothing here is memorised for this exact question; every phrase can be reused for a different advice task.

## Linking phrases that make you sound fluent

Examiners listen for how smoothly ideas connect. Learn a few and use them naturally:

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Adding | *and another thing is…*, *on top of that*, *what is more* |
| Contrast | *however*, *on the other hand*, *even though* |
| Reason | *because*, *that is why*, *the reason is…* |
| Example | *for instance*, *a good example is…* |
| Result | *as a result*, *so*, *which means that…* |
| Ending | *so overall*, *that is why I would…* |

Do not use all of them in every answer. Three or four well-placed links sound better than ten. For more detail see our guide to [linking words](/en/blog/linking-words-for-ielts-writing/); many apply to speaking too.

## What to do when you lose a word

Everyone does it. Do not stop; rephrase:

- *"What I mean is…"*
- *"It is kind of like…"*
- *"I cannot think of the exact word, but it is something you use for…"*

Rephrasing shows vocabulary skill and keeps the clock running in your favour.

## Pronunciation and delivery

You do not need a native accent. Work on **word stress**, clear endings and **pauses between ideas**. Speak at a steady pace: too fast makes mistakes, too slow wastes time. Read our guides to [connected speech](/en/blog/connected-speech-in-english/) and [word stress rules](/en/blog/english-word-stress-rules/) for the most useful sound patterns. ## Self-check

1. Complete the advice: *"If I ____ you, I would call the landlord first."*
2. Which phrase describes a picture: *"It looks like…"* or *"It is sure that…"*?
3. Give a polite request: *"I was ____ if you could change my appointment."*
4. Name two linking phrases for adding an idea.
5. What should you do if you forget a word?

### Answers

1. **were** (*If I were you, I would…*).
2. **It looks like…** It describes what you see without claiming certainty.
3. **wondering** (*I was wondering if you could…*).
4. For example, *on top of that* and *what is more*.
5. Rephrase: *"What I mean is…"* and keep speaking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I memorise a template for CELPIP speaking?

Memorise phrases, not whole answers. A full template often does not fit the question and sounds unnatural.

### How long should my answers be?

Use most of the time you are given, but finish your idea cleanly. A short closing sentence is better than being cut off.

### Do I need a Canadian accent?

No. Clear pronunciation, steady pace and natural language matter more than any particular accent.

### Is CELPIP the only test for Canadian immigration?

No. IELTS General Training is also accepted for many programmes. Check the official Canadian government pages for what applies to your application.
