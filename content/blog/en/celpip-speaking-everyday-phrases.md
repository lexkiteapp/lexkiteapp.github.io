Speaking to a screen with a countdown timer is unfamiliar for most people. In the Canadian English Language Proficiency Index Program (CELPIP General), test-takers complete eight speaking tasks ([celpip.ca](https://celpip.ca)). Having a few flexible CELPIP speaking phrases ready helps you start quickly and keep going; these are the everyday expressions that fit each task, from advice openers such as *If I were in your position, I would...* to scene and opinion phrases.

Rather than memorizing whole sentences, learn flexible signposts and swap in your own nouns and details, because a learned sentence like "In my opinion, the biggest benefit of this is..." sounds stiff if you recite it unchanged. Whether you are advising a friend on finding an apartment, describing a busy community park scene, or arguing for telecommuting policies in an opinion survey, these phrases give structure to your spontaneous thoughts.

## Natural CELPIP speaking phrases for each task

CELPIP Speaking tests practical, everyday communication rather than academic theory. Each task has a different purpose, so the phrases change:

{{diagram: d1 | Overview of core CELPIP speaking tasks, objectives, and natural phrasing.}}

### Task 1: Giving Advice (Warm, practical guidance)
You advise a friend, neighbour, or colleague facing a dilemma (e.g., choosing a daycare, buying winter gear, moving neighbourhoods).
- *Opening:* "Hi Sarah, I heard you're thinking about moving closer to downtown. If I were in your position, I would look at..."
- *Offering alternatives:* "Another option you might want to look into is..."
- *Addressing concerns:* "Don't worry too much about the higher rent, because you'll save significantly on transit passes."
- *Closing:* "Anyway, let me know what you decide over the weekend, and good luck with the search!"

### Task 2: Talking about a Personal Experience (Past narrative)
You narrate an event from your past (e.g., a challenging trip, an influential teacher, a celebration).
- *Setting the scene:* "One experience that immediately comes to mind occurred a few years ago when..."
- *Developing tension:* "To make matters more complicated, our flight was delayed by over four hours..."
- *Reflecting:* "Looking back on it now, that experience taught me the value of remaining calm under pressure."

### Tasks 3 and 4: Describing a Scene and Making Predictions (Spatial visualization)
You describe an everyday illustration (a bustling street market, a hospital waiting room) and predict what will happen next.
- *Foreground:* "On the left, in front, there's a young family, and they seem to be..."
- *Background:* "Towards the upper right corner, slightly in the background, we can see a courier delivering parcels..."
- *Predicting:* "Judging by the dark clouds gathering overhead, it seems very likely that the outdoor market stalls will soon pack up."

## High-impact phrases for opinion and workplace tasks

Tasks 5 through 8 ask you to compare, handle a difficult situation, give an opinion and describe something unusual, which are all situations you can meet at work:

### Task 5: Comparing and Persuading
You choose between two options (e.g., two holiday packages or two office coffee machines) and persuade a colleague or family member why your choice is superior.
- *Opening:* "Hi Michael, I took a close look at both options for our department's new software, and I really think Option B is the way to go."
- *Comparing features:* "While Option A is slightly less expensive upfront, Option B includes technical support and more storage."
- *Refuting counter-arguments:* "I realize you might be concerned about the initial setup time, but in the long run, the automated reporting features will save our team dozens of hours each month."

### Task 6: Dealing with a Difficult Situation
You handle an awkward situation politely, for example asking a neighbour to turn the music down or explaining a delay to a manager.
- *Softening the request:* "I'm really sorry to bring this up, but I wonder if we could find a solution together."
- *Explaining the problem:* "The issue is that the delivery hasn't arrived, and the client expects it by Friday."
- *Proposing a fix:* "What if I send you an update first thing tomorrow, so you can plan around it?"

### Task 7: Expressing Opinions on Social and Civic Issues
You express and defend an opinion on a community or policy question (e.g., whether public transit should be free, or if remote work should be mandatory).
- *Stating your stance:* "I think flexible hours are better than a fixed nine-to-five, mainly because people waste less time commuting."
- *Providing primary support:* "The biggest reason is the commute: people save an hour or two a day, and they arrive less stressed."
- *Acknowledging counter-arguments:* "Some managers worry about teamwork, but video calls have worked fine for most teams I know."
- *Concession and conclusion:* "So overall, I'd choose flexible hours, as long as everyone is available for a few core hours each day."

### Task 8: Describing an Unusual Situation
You describe a picture in which something does not look ordinary and explain what makes it odd.
- *Setting it up:* "The picture shows a car parked across two spaces, and the driver is carrying a ladder."
- *Why it is odd:* "What makes this unusual is that nobody seems to be in a hurry, even though the car is blocking the entrance."
- *Explaining to someone who was not there:* "If I were telling a friend, I would say it looks like a delivery went wrong."

Whatever the task, the sentences around your phrases still need to be accurate, so keep the [English grammar hub](/en/grammar/) open for quick checks on tenses and prepositions.

## Managing timing and vocal pacing under the digital countdown

A common problem in CELPIP Speaking is running out of time before you finish your thoughts, or running out of points with twenty seconds still on the clock.

Most tasks give 60 seconds of speaking time, while Tasks 1 and 7 give 90. Preparation time is 30 seconds for Tasks 1 to 4, 7 and 8, and 60 seconds for Tasks 5 and 6. Use the preparation time to pick your opening phrase and one example, not to write sentences.

For a 60-second task, aim for these milestones, and add the extra 30 seconds to your supporting points on Tasks 1 and 7:
1. **0:00–0:10 (The Setup):** Deliver a clear, confident opening sentence framing your purpose or point of view.
2. **0:10–0:35 (First Major Point):** State your primary reason and develop it with a concrete example or vivid descriptive detail.
3. **0:35–0:50 (Second Point / Counter-Perspective):** Introduce your secondary reason or contrastive observation using natural transitional markers (*On top of that...*, *Also...*).
4. **0:50–1:00 (The Wrap-up):** Conclude cleanly (*So all things considered, that's why I think...*). Never allow the recording to cut you off mid-word.

## Common candidate errors in CELPIP Speaking

1. **Speaking with excessive speed:** Rushing leads to slurred consonants and loss of breath. Clear articulation, a steady rhythm and audible grouping of ideas help a listener far more than fast delivery, and [sentence stress](/en/blog/sentence-stress-in-english-speaking-guide/) is the quickest way to practise that.
2. **Repeating generic adjectives:** Overusing *good*, *bad*, and *nice*. Upgrade to words such as *helpful*, *convenient*, *stressful* or *crowded*.
3. **Ignoring the prompt audience:** Speaking too casually to a manager in a work scenario such as Task 6, or sounding stiff and overly formal when advising a sibling in Task 1. Match your register to the relationship.
4. **Freezing during silence:** If your mind goes blank, buy a second of processing time with natural conversational fillers: *Well, looking at this picture more closely...* or *Let's see, another aspect worth noting is...*

Record yourself answering one prompt a day, play it back, and check whether you finished before the timer ended; our [daily English habits](/en/blog/daily-english-habits-for-ielts/) show one way to build the routine.

## Self-check: natural spoken phrasing

1. Which opening phrase is appropriate when advising a friend in CELPIP Task 1?
2. In Task 3, how should you spatially organize your description of an illustration?
3. How long do you speak in CELPIP Task 7?
4. How do you avoid being cut off at the end of a task?

**Answers**
1. *If I were in your position, I would...* or *Have you thought about...*
2. **From foreground to background, or left to right**, using explicit spatial prepositions.
3. **90 seconds.** Tasks 1 and 7 give 90 seconds; most other tasks give 60.
4. **Watch the on-screen digital timer** and begin your final wrap-up sentence when 10 seconds remain.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I pause or re-record my answer in CELPIP Speaking?

No. Once the preparation timer finishes, the microphone records automatically until the time expires. You cannot pause, restart, or edit your spoken response (check the CELPIP practice tutorial on celpip.ca for the current screen behaviour).

### Do I need a Canadian accent to achieve CLB 9+ in CELPIP?

No. Raters look at content, vocabulary, how easy you are to listen to, and task fulfilment. Clear, easy-to-follow pronunciation matters more than which accent you have.

### What should I do if I finish speaking before the timer runs out?

If you have completed your thought and still have 3 to 5 seconds remaining, simply stop with a natural closing remark (*...and that covers everything I wanted to share*) instead of padding with filler or sitting in awkward silence.
