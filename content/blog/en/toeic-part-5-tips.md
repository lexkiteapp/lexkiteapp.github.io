**TOEIC Part 5** gives you 30 incomplete sentences, each with four options. It is the first part of the Reading section, and it is where you should *gain* time: the faster and more accurately you finish Part 5, the more minutes you keep for the long texts in Part 7. This guide shows the five question types, a 20-second method for each item and the grammar clues that decide most answers.

## TOEIC Part 5 in the Reading section

The Reading section has 100 questions in 75 minutes: Part 5 (30 incomplete sentences), Part 6 (16 text-completion items) and Part 7 (54 reading comprehension questions). Our [TOEIC test format guide](/en/blog/toeic-test-format/) explains every part in detail.

Many teachers suggest a target of around 10 minutes for Part 5, or about 20 seconds per item. That is ambitious at first but realistic once you stop reading every sentence in full. Part 7 is long, and test takers who run out of time usually lose those points at the end of the paper.

## The 20-second method

{{diagram: d1 | Classify the question from the options before reading the sentence, then read only the part that decides the answer}}

The key habit: **look at the options before the sentence.** The four choices tell you what kind of question it is, and that tells you how much of the sentence you actually need to read.

- If the options are **one word in four forms** (*decide, decision, decisive, decisively*), it is a word-form question. Usually you only need the two or three words around the gap.
- If they are **four forms of one verb**, check the subject, time words and whether the action is active or passive.
- If they are **four different words with different meanings**, it is vocabulary; you need the whole sentence.

## The five question types in TOEIC Part 5

{{diagram: d2 | Each question type has its own clue: the options show you the type, the type shows you where to look}}

### 1. Word form (part of speech)

These are often the fastest points. Identify what the gap needs:

- After an article or possessive, before nothing or a preposition → **noun**: *the **decision** to expand*.
- Before a noun → **adjective**: *a **successful** launch*.
- Modifying a verb, an adjective or a whole clause → **adverb**: *grew **rapidly***, ***extremely** busy*.
- After *be*, *become*, *seem* → usually an **adjective**: *The results were **impressive***.

Watch suffixes: *-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity* (nouns); *-ful, -ive, -able, -ous* (adjectives); *-ly* (adverbs, but note *friendly* and *costly* are adjectives).

### 2. Verb form

Check three things in order:

1. **Subject–verb agreement:** *The list of candidates **is** ready* (the subject is *list*, singular).
2. **Time:** *since 2020* or *for three years* → present perfect; *last week* → past simple; *by next Friday* → often future perfect (*will have completed*). Our article on [present perfect vs past simple](/en/blog/present-perfect-vs-past-simple/) covers these signals.
3. **Voice:** does the subject do the action or receive it? *The report **was reviewed** by the team.* The [passive voice guide](/en/blog/passive-voice-in-english/) explains every form.

After certain verbs, you need a specific pattern: *recommend **doing***, *agree **to do***, *look forward to **doing***. These patterns are listed in [gerunds and infinitives](/en/blog/gerunds-and-infinitives/).

### 3. Connectors and prepositions

The deciding question: **is the gap followed by a clause (subject + verb) or a noun phrase?**

| Followed by a clause | Followed by a noun or -ing |
|---|---|
| although, even though | despite, in spite of |
| because, since | because of, due to |
| while | during |
| unless, if | without |

*Although the price rose, sales increased* versus *Despite the price rise, sales increased*. Then check meaning: contrast, cause, condition or time.

Simple prepositions also appear, often in fixed business phrases: *in advance*, *on time*, *at your earliest convenience*, *by Friday* (no later than). See [prepositions in, on, at](/en/blog/prepositions-in-on-at/) for time and place rules.

### 4. Pronouns

Decide the role: subject (*they*), object (*them*), possessive before a noun (*their*), possessive alone (*theirs*), or reflexive (*themselves*), used when the subject and object are the same person or for emphasis: *Ms Lee prepared the slides **herself***. A common trap: *by + reflexive* means alone: *He completed the audit **by himself***.

### 5. Vocabulary and collocation

When all four options are the same part of speech with different meanings, read the whole sentence and look for **collocations**: *meet a deadline*, *place an order*, *conduct a survey*, *address a concern*, *reach an agreement*. Similar-looking words are a classic trap (*affect/effect*, *rise/raise*); our list of [commonly confused words](/en/blog/commonly-confused-words-in-english/) covers many of them.

Vocabulary items take the longest. If you are unsure after 30 seconds, choose your best guess, mark it and move on; there is no penalty for wrong answers on the TOEIC.

## Worked examples

**Example A.** *The marketing team worked ____ to finish the campaign before the launch.*
(A) diligent (B) diligence (C) diligently (D) more diligent
→ Options = word forms. The gap describes how they *worked* (a verb) → adverb → **(C)**. You did not need to read *to finish the campaign before the launch*.

**Example B.** *By the time the auditors arrive, the finance department ____ all the documents.*
(A) prepares (B) will have prepared (C) has prepared (D) prepared
→ Verb forms. *By the time* + a future event → an action complete before that future point → future perfect → **(B)**.

**Example C.** *Please submit your expense claims ____ the end of the month.*
(A) until (B) by (C) since (D) during
→ Preposition. A deadline (no later than) → **(B)**. *Until* describes a continuing situation: *The office is open until six.*

## Common traps in TOEIC Part 5

- **Reading the whole sentence for every item.** For word-form questions, it wastes time and adds confusion.
- **Choosing a word because it "sounds right".** Check the grammar role first; then sound.
- **Ignoring the subject in long sentences.** In *The results of the survey conducted last spring ____*, the subject is *results* (plural).
- **Missing the article clue.** *a / an / the* before the gap often means a noun or an adjective + noun. Brush up with [a, an, the rules](/en/blog/articles-a-an-the-rules/).

## Self-check

Choose the best option. Try to spend no more than 20 seconds on each.

1. *The manager gave a ____ presentation to the board.* (persuade / persuasive / persuasively / persuasion)
2. *Sales ____ steadily since the new store opened.* (rose / have risen / rise / are rising)
3. *____ the delay, the shipment arrived in good condition.* (Although / Despite / Because / So)
4. *Mr Kato asked that the files be sent directly to ____.* (he / his / him / himself)
5. *The company plans to ____ a survey of customer satisfaction.* (make / conduct / do / take)
6. *All visitors must ____ at the front desk.* (register / registering / registered / be registering)

### Answers

1. **persuasive** – an adjective before the noun *presentation*.
2. **have risen** – *since* + a past point → present perfect.
3. **Despite** – followed by a noun phrase (*the delay*).
4. **him** – an object pronoun after the preposition *to*. *Himself* is wrong because *Mr Kato* is not the subject of the clause *the files be sent…*.
5. **conduct** – the collocation is *conduct a survey*.
6. **register** – base form after the modal *must*.

Five or six correct within two minutes is a strong pace. If verb forms caused errors, spend a week on tense and voice before doing more full tests.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions are in TOEIC Part 5?

There are 30 incomplete-sentence questions in Part 5. Together with Part 6 (16) and Part 7 (54), they make up the 100 questions of the Reading section.

### How much time should I spend on Part 5?

A common target is about 10 minutes, roughly 20 seconds per question. Word-form items can take less; vocabulary items may take a little more. The aim is to leave enough time for Part 7.

### Is it better to guess or leave a question blank?

Guess. The TOEIC Listening and Reading test does not deduct points for wrong answers, so a blank answer can only lose you a chance at the point.

### What grammar topics appear most often in Part 5?

Word forms (parts of speech), verb tense and voice, connectors and prepositions, pronouns, and vocabulary collocations come up again and again. Training to recognise these types from the options is the fastest way to improve.
