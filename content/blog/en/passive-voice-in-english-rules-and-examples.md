In English composition and formal speech, the passive voice is an indispensable grammatical instrument. Rather than highlighting the individual performing an action, it refocuses the reader's attention onto the recipient, the outcome, or the underlying phenomenon. In competitive examinations like the IELTS, TOEFL, and university academic writing, knowing the **passive voice in english rules** allows candidates to maintain objectivity, vary clause structures, and eliminate informal personal pronouns.

This comprehensive guide breaks down the core mechanics of passive voice transformations across all standard tenses, explores advanced impersonal passive structures, and illustrates best practices for academic essays.

## Why and when to employ the passive voice in english rules

While conversational English prioritizes the active voice for speed and directness, academic and scientific discourse demands the passive voice in specific stylistic circumstances:

1. **When the recipient or the consequence is more important than the actor**:  
   *Example*: "The historic peace treaty **was ratified** late yesterday evening." (The treaty itself takes precedence over the diplomatic signatories).
2. **When the agent is unknown, obvious, or intentionally unspecified**:  
   *Example*: "Valuable archaeological relics **were stolen** from the municipal museum." (The identity of the perpetrators remains unestablished).
3. **To sustain scientific neutrality and academic tone**:  
   *Example*: "The solution **was heated** gradually until vaporization commenced." (Eliminates personal subjectivity like *"We heated the liquid"*).

## Complete passive voice formulation across English tenses

The invariable principle of passive transformation dictates that **the direct object of the active clause becomes the new grammatical subject, the auxiliary verb 'to be' is conjugated to match the active tense, and the main verb converts into its past participle form (V3/ed)**.

| Tense / Construction | Active Voice Structure | Passive Voice Formula | Contextual Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Simple** | S + V(s/es) + O | **S + am/is/are + V3/ed** | "Vast quantities of electronic waste **are recycled** annually." |
| **Present Continuous** | S + am/is/are + V-ing + O | **S + am/is/are + being + V3/ed** | "A sustainable mass transit network **is being developed**." |
| **Present Perfect** | S + have/has + V3/ed + O | **S + have/has + been + V3/ed** | "Substantial fiscal reserves **have been allocated** for healthcare." |
| **Past Simple** | S + V2/ed + O | **S + was/were + V3/ed** | "The original laboratory facility **was destroyed** in the fire." |
| **Past Continuous** | S + was/were + V-ing + O | **S + was/were + being + V3/ed** | "The experimental parameters **were being monitored** continuously." |
| **Past Perfect** | S + had + V3/ed + O | **S + had + been + V3/ed** | "The commercial patent **had been registered** prior to publication." |
| **Future Simple (will)**| S + will + V-bare + O | **S + will + be + V3/ed** | "Detailed clinical conclusions **will be revealed** next month." |
| **Future (going to)** | S + is/are going to + V + O| **S + is/are going to be + V3/ed** | "Strict emissions caps **are going to be enforced** shortly." |
| **Modal Verbs** | S + modal + V-bare + O | **S + modal + be + V3/ed** | "Hazardous chemicals **must be stored** in sealed containers." |
| **Perfect Modals** | S + modal + have + V3/ed + O| **S + modal + have been + V3/ed**| "The missing specimens **should have been preserved** at sub-zero temperatures." |

*(Note: Continuous perfect tenses such as Present Perfect Continuous do theoretically possess passive forms, but they are almost universally avoided in natural prose due to clumsy phrasing).*

## Advanced passive structures for academic excellence

To achieve Band 7.5 or higher in writing tests, candidates should master these four specialized passive patterns:

### 1. Impersonal passive with reporting verbs
Verbs of cognition and assertion (*believe, claim, argue, consider, demonstrate, expect, report*) can be rendered impersonally to present viewpoints neutrally:

- **Pattern A (Dummy subject 'It')**:  
  `It + is/was + V3/ed (believed/acknowledged) + that-clause`  
  *Example*: "It is widely acknowledged that continuous urban sprawl strains municipal wastewater systems."
- **Pattern B (Subject raising with to-infinitive)**:  
  `S + is/are/was/were + V3/ed + to-infinitive / to have V3/ed`  
  *Simultaneous action*: "Solar photovoltaic panels **are estimated to generate** clean energy for over twenty-five years."  
  *Prior completed action*: "The ancient monument **is thought to have served** astronomical ceremonies."

### 2. Causative passive constructions
Used when an individual arranges for an external specialist or mechanism to carry out a service:
- `have + object + past participle`
- `get + object + past participle`  
*Example*: "The municipal government **had the historic bridge reinforced** by structural engineers."

### 3. Passives with ditransitive verbs (Two Objects)
Verbs accepting both an indirect and a direct object (*give, award, grant, offer, lend, send*) generate two distinct passive options:
- *Active*: "The university awarded Professor Higgins a substantial research grant."
- *Option 1 (Indirect object promoted - more common)*: "Professor Higgins **was awarded** a substantial research grant by the university."
- *Option 2 (Direct object promoted)*: "A substantial research grant **was awarded to** Professor Higgins by the university."

### 4. Perception verbs in the passive voice
When sensory verbs (*see, hear, watch, observe*) convert to the passive voice, the bare infinitive transforms into a full infinitive (*to-infinitive*):
- *Active*: "Investigators saw the driver abandon the vehicle."
- *Passive*: "The driver **was seen to abandon** the vehicle by investigators."

## Strategic implementation in IELTS Academic Writing

In IELTS Academic Task 1, describing a manufacturing process, an agricultural sequence, or a recycling cycle mandates passive verb dominance. Because materials and machinery do not operate themselves, human intervention remains backgrounded:
- *Example Task 1*: "Initially, the harvested tea leaves **are withered** on shallow trays, after which they **are rolled** and **subjected** to warm oxidation."

In Task 2 discursive essays, skillful alternation between active and passive verbs establishes a commanding academic rhythm, completely sidelining personal pronouns like *"I"*, *"we"*, or *"people"*:
- *Instead of writing*: *"People should consider the moral consequences of genetic engineering."*
- *Write*: *"The ethical ramifications of genetic engineering **must be deliberated** with profound caution by governing bodies."*

## When to retain or omit the 'by-phrase' agent

A recurrent challenge in academic prose is determining whether to include the agent (*by + noun phrase*):

- **Omit the agent when it is indefinite or uninformative**: Words like *by someone*, *by people*, or *by them* should almost always be deleted.  
  *Weak*: "The legislative reform was passed by lawmakers."  
  *Strong*: "The legislative reform was passed after intense parliamentary debate."
- **Retain the agent when the specific actor adds vital evidential weight**: If the identity of the researcher, institution, or historical figure validates the claim, keep the agent.  
  *Example*: "The empirical hypothesis **was rigorously tested by researchers at Stanford University**."
- **Retain the agent to place heavy information at the end of the clause (End-Weight Principle)**: In academic English, long, complex noun phrases are naturally positioned at the end of sentences for better readability.  
  *Example*: "The revolutionary theory of relativity **was formulated by Albert Einstein**, who revolutionized modern physics."

## Common errors to eradicate

| Frequent Mistake | Incorrect Example | Correct Academic Transformation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Omitting the auxiliary verb 'be'** | "The experiment conducted yesterday." | "The experiment **was conducted** yesterday." |
| **Passivizing intransitive verbs** | "The economic crisis was happened unexpectedly." | "The economic crisis **happened** unexpectedly." *(Intransitive verbs cannot be passive)* |
| **Incorrect past participle form** | "The report was wrote by consultants." | "The report was **written** by consultants." |
| **Redundant by-agent phrases** | "The highway was repaired by workers." | "The highway was **repaired** within three weeks." *(Omit obvious or vague agents)* |
