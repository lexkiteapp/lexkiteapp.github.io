Most people who ask about **IELTS for UKVI** have the same worry: they already know that IELTS exists, and now a UK visa page says they must take a specific version. The short explanation is that IELTS for UKVI is IELTS delivered as a Secure English Language Test (SELT), which means it is taken with an approved provider at an approved centre so that the UK Home Office can accept the result. This guide explains who might need it, how it differs from the standard test, what the main versions are, and how to book without wasting money.

This is language-test guidance, not legal advice. We read the GOV.UK pages on Skilled Worker English requirements and on Secure English Language Tests on 5 November 2026 for our [IELTS for immigration guide](/en/blog/ielts-for-immigration-guide/), and the details below follow that review plus the test provider's public descriptions. Immigration rules change often, so confirm every point on the official pages for your own visa.

## What IELTS for UKVI is, and what a SELT means

The Home Office requires some visa applicants to prove their English with a **Secure English Language Test**. GOV.UK names IELTS for UKVI as one of the approved tests and says you can only take a SELT with an approved provider at an approved centre. Results are valid for two years from the date they are awarded, on the guidance we reviewed.

In practice, the tasks in IELTS for UKVI are the same kind as in the standard test, and scores use the same 0 to 9 band scale. What changes is the **security and the paperwork**: the test is delivered at approved centres, the result form carries a UKVI reference that the Home Office can check, and test providers describe additional identity and recording measures. Details such as exact recording rules and prices are set by the providers, so check them when you book instead of relying on a general description.

The important consequence: **a standard IELTS result is not a SELT.** If you took a standard test for general purposes, it may not count for a visa that requires a SELT, even if the band scores look high enough.

## Who might need IELTS for UKVI

It depends on the visa route. Examples from the pages we reviewed:

- **Skilled Worker:** applicants must prove English at least at CEFR level B2 in reading, writing, speaking and listening, unless they are exempt. They can do this through a SELT, among other ways. Whether a SELT is needed for you depends on your nationality, your qualifications and the exemptions on the page.
- **Partner, parent or settlement applications:** these may need only Speaking and Listening, which changes which test you book.
- **Study routes:** some university applications use IELTS for UKVI (Academic) when the university or the visa process asks for a SELT; others accept other evidence.

Because rules differ by route and change over time, do not rely on a friend's experience. Find the guidance page for your visa, and look for words like "approved Secure English Language Test", "CEFR level" and the list of approved providers.

## The main versions: Academic, General Training and Life Skills

{{diagram: d1 | A summary of the usual options. The version and level required depend on your route, so confirm on GOV.UK.}}

**IELTS for UKVI Academic** follows the Academic format: academic reading texts and a Task 1 that describes a chart, table or process. It is typically associated with study at degree level or professional registration.

**IELTS for UKVI General Training** follows the General Training format: everyday and workplace reading texts and a letter for Task 1. It is typically associated with work and migration routes.

**IELTS Life Skills** tests only **Speaking and Listening** and is reported as a pass or fail rather than a band score. Test providers describe levels such as A1 and B1, which relate to the CEFR scale, and the level you need depends on your visa category (for example, some family routes and some settlement or citizenship routes). It is a different, shorter test, so make sure that is what your route asks for.

If you are unsure whether you need Academic or General Training, see our guide to [IELTS General vs Academic for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-general-vs-academic-immigration/).

## How to book IELTS for UKVI without mistakes

{{diagram: d2 | Four checks before you pay. A booking error is the most expensive one.}}

1. **Read the route guidance.** On GOV.UK, find the page for your visa and the section about English. Note the CEFR level and the skills required.
2. **Name the exact test.** Write down which UKVI test and which version (Academic, General Training, or Life Skills), and the level the route asks for.
3. **Choose an approved provider and centre.** Use the list on GOV.UK or the provider's own official site. A centre that offers standard IELTS may not offer the UKVI version.
4. **Book the UKVI option.** When you register, select the UKVI version explicitly. Check the name on your booking matches your passport, because identity checks apply.
5. **Count the dates.** Work backwards from your application date so that the result is still valid and ready. Allow time for results and for a retake if needed.
6. **Keep documents.** Save your booking confirmation, the result and the reference number in a safe place.

Test fees, result times and available dates differ by country and centre. Check the provider's page for current information rather than relying on old figures.

## Scores, levels and what B2 means

The route may ask for a CEFR level (for example B2) rather than a band. Providers publish how IELTS bands relate to CEFR levels, and the Home Office states the minimum in the guidance for each route. Use the official numbers for your route and your visa, and do not rely on a general "band to level" chart from a blog post. Check each skill separately: some routes require the level in all four skills, so one low skill may cause a failure even if the others are strong.

## How to prepare for IELTS for UKVI

Because the tasks are like the standard test, normal IELTS preparation applies. The main differences are logistical, not academic. A sensible plan:

1. **Find your level.** A short placement test and a timed practice show which skill is lowest.
2. **Target the weakest skill.** If Writing is the lowest skill, practise essays and get feedback; if it is Listening, practise daily dictation. Our guide to [IELTS listening tips](/en/blog/ielts-listening-tips/) and our [IELTS speaking Part 1 tips](/en/blog/ielts-speaking-part-1-tips/) are good starting points.
3. **Get used to the test setting.** Practise speaking aloud with a timer, in a quiet place, because Speaking is recorded in many SELT settings, according to providers.
4. **Plan the weeks.** Use your test date and weekly hours to set small goals. Our [IELTS self-study plan](/en/blog/ielts-self-study-plan/) can be adjusted for 8, 12 or 24 weeks.

### Answers

1. A **Secure English Language Test**, taken with an approved provider at an approved centre.
2. **No.** A standard IELTS result is not a SELT.
3. **Speaking and Listening**, reported as pass or fail.
4. **Two years** from the date the result is awarded, on the guidance we reviewed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is IELTS for UKVI harder than standard IELTS?

The tasks and band scale are of the same kind, so it is not designed to be harder. What differs is the delivery, security and paperwork. Prepare as you would for the standard test.

### Can I use a standard IELTS result for a UK visa?

Only where the guidance for your route accepts it. If the route requires a Secure English Language Test, a standard result will not count, so check before you book.

### Do I need Academic or General Training for UKVI?

It depends on the route. Study and professional routes often use Academic, and work and migration routes often use General Training. Confirm on the guidance for your visa.

### What is IELTS Life Skills?

A shorter test of Speaking and Listening only, reported as pass or fail at a level such as A1 or B1, used by some family, settlement or citizenship routes.
