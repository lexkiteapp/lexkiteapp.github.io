**IELTS summary completion** asks you to fill gaps in a short summary of part of the passage, using either words from the text or words from a list. The summary paraphrases the passage, so you cannot just look for the same words; you must find the idea, then identify the exact word that fits. Marks are lost for small things: one word too many, a plural missed, a spelling error. This guide gives a method, shows an original example, and explains the traps in both versions of the task.

## What IELTS summary completion looks like

You read a summary, usually a paragraph of three to six sentences, with numbered gaps. The instruction either says *Choose NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage for each answer* or gives you a box of words to choose from. The summary covers a section of the text, often one or two paragraphs, and the gaps follow the order of the text. This is one of several task types; for an overview of all of them, see [IELTS Reading question types](/en/blog/ielts-reading-question-types/).

## A method for each gap

{{diagram: d2 | Read, predict, locate, match, check}}

1. **Read the whole summary first.** It tells you the topic and the logic. Often the first sentence also tells you which paragraph to find.
2. **Predict the word class for each gap.** Is it a noun, a verb, an adjective, a number? The words before and after the gap show this.
3. **Locate the matching part of the passage.** The summary follows the order of the text. If gap 1 is in paragraph B, gap 2 is later in paragraph B or in paragraph C.
4. **Match meaning, not words.** The summary uses a paraphrase of the passage. Find the sentence with the same idea.
5. **Check the limit and the spelling.** Copy the word exactly from the passage, and count your words.

## An original passage and summary

> *Tidal energy is generated by the movement of seawater caused by the gravitational pull of the moon. Unlike wind or solar power, tides are highly predictable, because their timing can be calculated years in advance. However, the construction of tidal barrages is expensive, and critics point out that they can damage the habitats of birds and fish in estuaries. For this reason, several countries are testing underwater turbines, which are cheaper to install and have less impact on the surrounding environment.*

Summary: *Tides result from the pull of the (1) ____ . They are more (2) ____ than wind or sun, but barrages are costly and may harm (3) ____ . Some countries therefore test (4) ____ , which are less expensive.*

Instruction: *Choose NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS from the passage for each answer.*

**Answers:**
1. **moon**. The pull of the moon is stated directly.
2. **predictable**. *Highly predictable* is in the passage; the summary says *more predictable*, so the missing word is the adjective.
3. **habitats** (the passage says *damage the habitats of birds and fish*; *habitats* fits *harm* and is a noun).
4. **underwater turbines** (two words, within the limit).

Notice that the summary reworded *expensive* as *costly*, and *damage* as *harm*. This is why you must follow the ideas rather than search for the same words.

## Predict the word class

Grammar is a powerful clue, especially for the word-bank version. Look at the words around the gap:

| Before or after the gap | Likely word class |
|---|---|
| *a / an / the ____* | noun or adjective |
| *is / are ____* (before a noun) | adjective or *-ing* participle |
| *to ____* | verb |
| *more ____ than* | adjective |
| *____ of* | noun |
| *can ____* | verb |

In the word-bank version, sort the words in the box into verbs, nouns and adjectives before you start. Then each gap has only two or three possibilities. The box always has more words than gaps, so some words are distractors that may fit grammatically but not in meaning.

## The word-limit rules

- *NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS* means one or two words. Three words is wrong.
- *NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER* means you can add a figure.
- A hyphenated word normally counts as one word.
- Numbers can be written as digits or words if the passage does the same.
- You must copy the word as it appears: *habitat* is wrong if the passage says *habitats* and the sentence needs the plural.

If the summary says *the ____ of birds and fish* and the passage says *the habitats of birds and fish*, the gap needs a plural noun. Always read the words after the gap.

## Common traps

| Trap | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Changing the form of a word | You think a singular sounds nicer | Copy the passage and check grammar |
| Choosing a word with similar meaning but not in the passage | You paraphrase the answer | The answer must come from the passage |
| Wrong paragraph | A keyword appears twice | Use the order of the text |
| Too many words | You copy a phrase | Check the limit |
| Misspelling | Rush at the end | Copy letter by letter |
| Answer in the wrong place | You find the right word but in a different sentence | Check the whole idea |

Practise your paraphrase recognition with [how to paraphrase in IELTS](/en/blog/how-to-paraphrase-in-ielts/), and use [skimming and scanning](/en/blog/ielts-reading-skimming-and-scanning/) to find sections quickly.

## Why the task is harder than it looks

Summary completion combines three skills at once: understanding a paraphrase, using grammar to predict a word, and copying precisely. Candidates who are strong in one skill often lose marks on another. A reader with excellent comprehension may write *habitat* instead of *habitats*, and a reader with perfect grammar may fail to match a paraphrase because they know too few synonyms. That is why the best preparation mixes reading practice with a small amount of vocabulary work each day. When you meet a paraphrase pair such as *expensive* and *costly*, write it in a notebook next to the topic, because the same pairs return in different passages.

## Time and order

Summary tasks can come at any point in a passage, so leave enough time. Many candidates aim for a few minutes per set of gaps once the method is familiar, but your pace will depend on the passage. Because the gaps follow the order of the text, you can complete them in order without jumping around. If one gap blocks you, leave it and return after the others; the surrounding answers often show the missing word.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do the gaps follow the order of the passage?

Usually, yes. The gaps in a summary follow the order of the information in the text, which narrows the area you need to search.

### Can I change the form of a word?

If the instruction says to choose words from the passage, copy them as they appear. Grammar must still fit the sentence, so check whether the gap needs a singular or plural form.

### What is the difference between summary and sentence completion?

A summary paraphrases a longer section of the passage, with several gaps in connected sentences. Sentence completion has separate sentences, each with its own gap.

### How do I avoid spelling mistakes?

Copy the word letter by letter from the passage. A correct answer with a wrong spelling is marked incorrect.
