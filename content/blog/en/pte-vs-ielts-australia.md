Australia accepts both PTE Academic (Pearson Test of English) and IELTS (International English Language Testing System) for skilled migration, so PTE vs IELTS for Australia comes down to computer marking versus a human examiner and which format suits you. PTE Academic runs in three parts totalling roughly 130 to 153 minutes (pearsonpte.com gives each part's length), while IELTS is a separate Listening, Reading and Writing sitting plus an 11 to 14 minute Speaking interview. The level-by-level IELTS path is in our [IELTS score for Australia PR](/en/blog/ielts-score-australia-pr/) guide; this article compares the two tests.

The Australian Department of Home Affairs ([immi.homeaffairs.gov.au](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-support/meeting-our-requirements/english-language)) accepts both tests for General Skilled Migration (subclasses 189, 190 and 491). For tests taken from 7 August 2025 its list also includes CELPIP General, MET and LanguageCert Academic (added from that date), alongside TOEFL iBT, OET and Cambridge C1 Advanced.

Both tests must be taken in person at a test centre, because at-home versions are not accepted. Home Affairs also says it accepts IELTS results that include One Skill Retake for eligible visas, so ask whether your subclass is one of them.

## Score levels for PTE vs IELTS for Australia

Points-tested skilled migration in Australia checks each skill separately. An exceptional mark in three sections will not compensate for a weak fourth section. The PTE numbers below apply to tests taken from 7 August 2025; tests taken before that date used a different, flat scale, so the same test name now carries different numbers. Check the date on your result and the current Home Affairs table before you rely on any figure.

PTE Academic scores run from 10 to 90 and IELTS bands from 0 to 9, so the numbers are not interchangeable; use the table, not a conversion guess.

## How the PTE Academic test is laid out

According to pearsonpte.com's test format page, PTE Academic is one computer test in three parts:

- **Part 1, Speaking and Writing:** 76 to 84 minutes and 9 question types.
- **Part 2, Reading:** 23 to 30 minutes and 5 question types. Reading and Writing: Fill in the blanks also assesses writing.
- **Part 3, Listening:** 31 to 39 minutes and 8 question types. Audio and video clips play automatically and are heard once; you may take notes.

IELTS instead splits Listening, Reading and Writing, taken together in one sitting, from the separate face-to-face Speaking interview, and its Writing paper alone is 60 minutes. PTE Academic is one computer session covering all four skills.

## Machine scoring vs human examiners: the core divide

The biggest difference is how Speaking and Writing are marked:

### Speaking Evaluation
- **IELTS Speaking:** You sit in a quiet room with a certified human examiner. The examiner can respond to natural pauses and follow context, although any human judgement carries some subjectivity.
- **PTE Academic Speaking:** You speak into a headset microphone in a shared room where other candidates are speaking at the same time. According to Pearson's test format guide, the section opens with Personal Introduction, which is not scored, and Read Aloud is the first scored speaking task. According to Pearson's published materials, scoring is largely automated, so your result comes mainly from AI scoring of fluency, pronunciation and content, with a human expert reviewing some responses, not from a live examiner talking with you.
- **What that means for you:** Scoring by software removes the variation between examiners, but the software scores what it records, so speak steadily at a natural pace and do not stop mid-task to restart. If background voices distract you, weigh that before you choose.

### Writing Evaluation
- **IELTS Writing:** A human examiner evaluates your ideas against the four official band descriptors. They assess task response, coherence, vocabulary and grammar, and memorised templates do not replace real content.
- **PTE Academic Writing:** Pearson describes the marking as AI scoring, with a human expert reviewing some responses, so what counts includes vocabulary, spelling, grammar and how well you address the prompt.

## Integrated scoring: how PTE tasks affect multiple skills

PTE Academic uses **integrated skill scoring**:

- In IELTS, each module is completely compartmentalised: Reading questions only affect your Reading band; Writing tasks only impact Writing.
- In PTE, some task types contribute marks to more than one skill.

So one weak task can affect more than one skill score, and a strong one can help two. Pearson's score guide shows which tasks feed more than one skill.

## Which test should you choose for Australia PR?

Use these lists as a rough guide, not a rule:

### Consider PTE Academic if:
1. You have fast, accurate typing skills and feel completely comfortable navigating computer interfaces.
2. Your speaking delivery is steady, fluent and clear when recorded.
3. You prefer your writing to be scored mainly by AI rather than judged by a live examiner.

### Consider IELTS if:
1. You prefer communicating with a real human being who can understand conversational context and natural body language.
2. You would rather talk with a real person than to a screen.
3. You write well-structured arguments but find timed typing under a countdown, as in Summarize Written Text, stressful.

If you lean towards IELTS, see how its two versions differ in [IELTS General vs Academic for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-general-vs-academic-immigration/). If you are also considering New Zealand, our guide to the [IELTS score for New Zealand residence](/en/blog/ielts-new-zealand-skilled-migrant/) lists its rules.

### A short checklist before you decide
- Which of the Home Affairs tables applies to the date on your result?
- Is a test centre available for the test you prefer? At-home versions are not accepted.
- Does your subclass allow IELTS One Skill Retake results, if that matters to you?
- Do you do better when software scores you from a recording, or when an examiner reacts to you?

Compare each score with the Proficient row: Listening 58, Reading 59, Writing 69, Speaking 76. Listening, Reading and Speaking pass, but Writing 68 is one point under 69, so the report does not reach Proficient English (10 points), however strong the others are. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I combine an IELTS score with a PTE Academic score for Australia PR?

Each table lists the scores for one test, so the four scores are read from that single test; Home Affairs does not describe mixing components from different tests, so check before you plan to. IELTS One Skill Retake results are accepted for eligible visas, so ask whether your subclass is one of them.

### Is PTE Academic easier than IELTS?

Neither test is easier in general. The scales are different, and which is easier depends on whether you do better with a human examiner or a computer. Try the free sample material from each test owner before you decide.

### How long are PTE Academic results valid for Australian immigration?

For points-tested General Skilled Migration (subclasses 189, 190, 491), Home Affairs accepts results from a test taken within three years before you apply; check the page for tests taken before 7 August 2025.
