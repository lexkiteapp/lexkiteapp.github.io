Australia accepts both Pearson Test of English (PTE) Academic and IELTS for many visa applications, so **PTE vs IELTS for Australia** is a choice between two formats, not between a good test and a bad one. The better choice is the one in which your weakest skill scores highest. This guide explains how the Department of Home Affairs describes English levels, what changed on 7 August 2025, how the two tests differ in format and scoring, and how to decide with your own evidence.

This is language-test guidance and not migration advice. We read Pearson's article on the Home Affairs requirements (dated 7 August 2025) on 15 November 2026, and our companion guide to [IELTS for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-for-immigration-guide/) reports what the department's pages said about IELTS. The Department's site blocked our automated check, so confirm every number on the live pages for your own subclass before you book.

## What changed on 7 August 2025

Home Affairs describes English ability in levels such as Competent, Proficient and Superior. Before 7 August 2025, PTE Academic needed a single score in each skill for each level: 50, 65 and 79 respectively. Pearson's article says new requirements **take effect from 7 August 2025** for anyone taking the test for Home Affairs visa purposes, and that if you took the test before that date the previous requirements still apply, even if you submit your application after 7 August.

The new requirements are different for each skill, which is why the old shorthand ("65 in each") is no longer right. Pearson says some requirements have increased and most have decreased.

{{diagram: d1 | PTE Academic numbers are from Pearson's table; IELTS levels are from the Home Affairs pages summarised in our immigration guide. L = Listening, R = Reading, S = Speaking, W = Writing.}}

Two cautions. First, the table shows the **level**, not the points a visa will give you: the points and the level you need differ between visas, so open your subclass page on the Home Affairs site. Second, the level depends on all your skills, and one low skill can hold the whole profile down. The department's own example for IELTS says the lowest component sets your level, even if the average looks higher.

## How PTE and IELTS differ in format

The points below are general descriptions of each test's design; confirm the current format with each provider when you book.

**PTE Academic** is taken entirely on a computer at a test centre, and the speaking is recorded by the computer and scored automatically. The test is organised in sections for Speaking and Writing together, then Reading, then Listening. Many tasks combine skills, so a single answer can influence more than one score: for example, reading a text aloud tests speaking and reading together.

**IELTS** has four separate papers: Listening, Reading, Writing and a Speaking interview with an examiner, usually face to face. Candidates choose between Academic and General Training depending on the visa, and in many places between paper and computer delivery. Tests delivered completely online, including IELTS Online, were not accepted by Home Affairs on the pages we reviewed.

| Point | PTE Academic | IELTS |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | Computer at a centre | Paper or computer at a centre |
| Speaking | Recorded and scored automatically | Interview with an examiner |
| Integration | Many tasks combine skills | Four separate parts |
| Result | Per-skill scores | Per-skill bands |

Because PTE is scored by a computer, some candidates feel it rewards clear, steady, accurate speech and does not penalise a nervous personality. Others dislike talking to a screen. In IELTS, a friendly examiner can help some candidates relax, while others find the interaction stressful. Both reactions are normal, which is why trying a sample of each is more reliable than opinions.

## PTE vs IELTS for Australia: a fair way to choose

{{diagram: d2 | Decide using evidence from your own timed practice, not by reputation or advice from forums.}}

1. **Look up your target.** For your visa, find the level required and the per-skill numbers on the official pages. Write them for each skill.
2. **Take a level check.** A short placement test shows whether you are far from your target level, so you know how many months of work are ahead.
3. **Try both formats.** Do a timed sample of each, using the official practice materials from Pearson and the IELTS partners. Include the format that scares you most.
4. **Compare by skill.** Which test gives you a better result in your weakest skill? That is the one to book.
5. **Check logistics.** Dates, prices and result times differ between cities. Check the test centre nearest to you and keep results validity in mind, since expired results will not count.

If you are close in both, choose by comfort. Test-day calm often matters more than a tiny scoring difference.

## Skills that help in both tests

Whichever test you choose, the skills beneath it are the same: understanding spoken English at natural speed, reading quickly for meaning, writing organised paragraphs, and speaking clearly in complete sentences.

- **Listening:** listen to short audio every day and write what you hear. Our guide to [English dictation practice](/en/blog/english-dictation-practice-guide/) shows how.
- **Reading:** practise getting the main idea quickly, then checking details. See [active reading strategies](/en/blog/active-reading-strategies-for-ielts/).
- **Writing:** write short paragraphs with a clear topic sentence and an example; our [linking words guide](/en/blog/linking-words-for-ielts-writing/) helps with flow.
- **Speaking:** practise clear pronunciation and a steady rhythm. The [pronunciation practice guide](/en/blog/how-to-practice-english-pronunciation/) covers stress and intonation.

## Common questions people ask before deciding

**Which one has the easier Speaking?** Neither is easier in general. PTE is computer-scored and has task types such as reading aloud, repeating a sentence and describing an image; IELTS is a conversation with three parts. Try both.

**Can I use the same result for other countries?** Sometimes. Each country sets its own list of accepted tests and its own scores, and some systems only accept the Academic or General Training version of IELTS. Check them one by one, as our [immigration guide](/en/blog/ielts-for-immigration-guide/) explains.

**Will a high overall score be enough?** Not necessarily. The levels are set by each skill, so a high overall can still fail a skill minimum.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which test is better for the Australian visa, PTE or IELTS?

Both are accepted for many visas, and neither is "better". Choose the one where your weakest skill performs best in practice, and check that it is accepted for your subclass.

### Are the PTE score requirements the same for every skill?

No. Since 7 August 2025 the table lists separate numbers for Listening, Reading, Speaking and Writing at each level, so do not use a single score for all four.

### What if I took PTE before August 2025?

Pearson's article says that if you took the test before 7 August 2025, the previous requirements still apply, even if you apply later. Check the Home Affairs page for your own case.

### Can I take either test online from home?

Do not assume so. Home Affairs did not accept fully online tests on the pages we reviewed for IELTS, and the rules can change, so confirm that your chosen format is accepted for your visa.
