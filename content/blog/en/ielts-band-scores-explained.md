To understand **IELTS band scores**, hold on to three facts. Each of the four skills (Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking) gets its own band from 0 to 9. The overall band is the average of those four, rounded to the nearest half band. And a band is a description of what you can do in English, not a percentage of questions answered correctly. Once you see how these pieces fit, you can set a realistic target, work out which skill is holding your overall down, and decide where the next study hour should go.

## What IELTS band scores really measure

Every candidate receives a band for each skill and one overall band. Scores run in half-band steps from 1 to 9, with 0 for someone who did not attempt the test. The descriptors are the same whichever version you take, although the tasks differ between Academic and General Training.

{{diagram: d1 | The whole bands in everyday words: the difference between neighbouring bands is real but never huge}}

A few practical points follow from this table:

- **Band 6 is not "failing".** It describes an effective user who still makes noticeable errors. Many courses accept it; many immigration routes want more.
- **Band 7 is the common threshold** for competitive university programmes and several professional registrations, but requirements differ by institution and country. Always read the current rules of the organisation you are applying to; they can change.
- **The gap between bands feels uneven.** Moving from 5.0 to 6.0 often means cleaning up basic accuracy. Moving from 7.0 to 8.0 means precision, range and naturalness across a whole performance.

Be careful with online claims that a band "equals" a certain vocabulary size or a certain number of study hours. Individual progress varies a lot with your starting point, how you practise and how much English you meet outside study. Treat any such figure as a rough guess, not a promise.

## How the overall band is calculated

The overall band is the mean of your four skill bands, rounded to the nearest half band. The rounding rule is the one people most often get wrong:

- An average ending in .25 rounds **up** to the next half band.
- An average ending in .75 rounds **up** to the next whole band.
- Any other average rounds to the nearest half band: .125 rounds down to .0, .375 and .625 round to .5, and .875 rounds up to the next whole band.

{{diagram: d2 | Four skill bands in, one rounded average out}}

Here are four original examples:

| Listening | Reading | Writing | Speaking | Average | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.0 | 7.0 | 6.0 | 6.5 | 6.625 | 6.5 |
| 7.5 | 7.0 | 6.0 | 6.5 | 6.75 | 7.0 |
| 6.5 | 6.5 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.25 | 6.5 |
| 6.5 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.125 | 6.0 |

Look at rows three and four. Both people have a 6.0 in Writing and Speaking, yet a small difference in Listening and Reading, which moves the average from 6.125 to 6.25, changes the overall by half a band. That is why a single small improvement in the right place can lift the whole result.

Remember also that many organisations ask for a minimum in each skill as well as an overall. Someone with 7.0 overall and 5.5 in Writing may not be accepted where "no skill below 6.5" is required. Your plan should protect your weakest skill, not just the average.

## Why Listening and Reading feel different from Writing and Speaking

Listening and Reading are marked by counting correct answers out of 40, and the count is converted to a band using a table that is published for the test. The conversion varies slightly between test versions, and the Reading table differs between Academic and General Training. As a very rough guide, around 30 correct answers in Listening points to band 7, while Reading needs a similar total for Academic and a higher one for General Training. Use the exact tables on official preparation material for serious planning.

Writing and Speaking are judged by trained examiners against four criteria each:

| Skill | The four criteria |
|---|---|
| Writing | Task achievement or response, coherence and cohesion, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy |
| Speaking | Fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, pronunciation |

Each criterion carries equal weight, and the final band is a combined judgement. Task 2 in Writing counts for twice as much as Task 1, so a strong Task 2 is the single most valuable piece of Writing preparation. The guides on [IELTS Writing Task 2 structure](/en/blog/ielts-writing-task-2-structure/) and [complex sentences for IELTS writing](/en/blog/complex-sentences-for-ielts-writing/) show how to earn those criteria one by one.

Because productive skills are criterion-based, they respond to specific habits: planning, paraphrase, linking, answer extension. If you are stuck between bands, [how to break the IELTS band plateau](/en/blog/how-to-break-ielts-band-plateau/) lists the usual reasons and fixes.

## How to set a realistic target band

A useful target is built backwards from the requirement, then checked against honest evidence of your current level.

1. **Write down the exact requirement.** Is it an overall score only, or an overall plus minimums? Which test version is accepted? How recent must the result be? Many results are only treated as valid for a limited period, so check the validity rule of your receiving organisation.
2. **Measure where you are now.** One full practice test is worth more than a dozen guesses. Take it under exam timing and mark Writing and Speaking critically, ideally with someone who knows the criteria.
3. **Find the gap per skill.** Subtract your current bands from the target and note the skill with the biggest gap. That skill needs the most hours; the skill closest to target needs maintenance.
4. **Pick a timeline you can keep.** A band of improvement in a skill often takes longer than people hope. A plan you can finish is better than a plan that looks impressive. For schedules see [IELTS self-study plan](/en/blog/ielts-self-study-plan/), and for immigration-specific scoring see [IELTS for immigration](/en/blog/ielts-for-immigration-guide/) and [IELTS to CLB conversion](/en/blog/ielts-clb-score-canada/).
5. **Re-test on a schedule.** Repeat a timed practice every few weeks and compare skill by skill, not just the overall number.

### An example of reading your own profile

Imagine a candidate who needs 7.0 overall with no skill below 6.5. After a practice test the skills come out as Listening 7.5, Reading 7.0, Writing 6.0 and Speaking 6.5. The average is 6.75, which rounds to 7.0, so the overall looks fine; but Writing at 6.0 breaks the minimum. For this candidate, the best investment is Writing, specifically Task 2 accuracy and structure, while Listening needs only light upkeep.

Answer these, then check the explanations below.

1. A candidate scores 6.5, 6.5, 7.0 and 6.0. What is the overall band?
2. Which criterion appears in both Writing and Speaking?
3. Task 2 is worth how much compared with Task 1 in Writing?
4. A friend says "band 7 means getting 70% of the answers right". What is wrong with that?

**Answers:**
1. The mean is 6.5, so the overall is **6.5**.
2. **Lexical resource** and **grammatical range and accuracy** appear in both.
3. Task 2 counts for **about twice as much** as Task 1.
4. Bands describe ability, and Listening and Reading use a published raw-score conversion that is not a simple percentage. Writing and Speaking are marked against criteria, not counted.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is the IELTS overall band score calculated?

Add the four skill bands, divide by four, and round to the nearest half band. An average ending in .25 rounds up to the next half band and one ending in .75 rounds up to the next whole band.

### Is band 6.5 a good IELTS score?

It depends on your goal. It is an effective level for many courses, but some universities, professions and immigration routes ask for 7.0 or higher, sometimes with a minimum in each skill. Check the current requirement for the exact programme or visa.

### Which IELTS skill is hardest to improve?

It varies by person. Many learners find Writing slow to improve because accuracy, structure and range must all rise together, while others struggle most with Speaking fluency. Your practice test results will show where your own gap is.

### Can I retake only one skill?

A One Skill Retake option has been introduced for some computer-delivered tests, but availability and rules differ by country and test centre and can change, so check the official IELTS site or ask your test centre before booking.
