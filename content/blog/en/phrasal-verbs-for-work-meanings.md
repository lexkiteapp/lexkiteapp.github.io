In modern corporate environments across the globe, business English is rarely spoken in stiff, Latinate prose. While formal written contracts still feature words like *investigate*, *postpone*, and *terminate*, daily office conversation, Zoom meetings, and Slack channels are powered by multi-word verbs.

Understanding **phrasal verbs for work meaning** bridges the gap between passive textbook English and spontaneous workplace fluency. Knowing how to *follow up* with a client, *walk through* a proposal, *iron out* contractual snags, and *wrap up* a meeting ensures you communicate with clarity and natural authority.

## Decoding phrasal verbs through particle logic

Rather than attempting to memorize hundreds of arbitrary verb combinations, understand the spatial and functional logic of the **particle** (preposition or adverb):

{{diagram: d1 | Core business particles, underlying meanings, and examples.}}

- **The Particle "UP" (Completion or Increase):**  
  *Wrap up* (complete a meeting), *scale up* (expand production), *catch up* (reach parity).
- **The Particle "OFF" (Delay, Removal, or Separation):**  
  *Put off* (postpone an event), *call off* (cancel an initiative), *lay off* (terminate employment due to restructuring).
- **The Particle "OUT" (Distribution, Exhaustion, or Clarification):**  
  *Roll out* (launch a new product), *sort out* (resolve confusion), *burn out* (exhaust energy reserves).
- **The Particle "THROUGH" (Full Completion from Start to Finish):**  
  *Walk through* (explain sequentially), *follow through* (execute a promise).

## 30 Essential phrasal verbs for work meaning and context

Study these thirty indispensable workplace phrasal verbs grouped by professional context:

### Category 1: Meetings and Project Coordination
1. **Follow up (on):** To pursue a matter further or check on progress.  
   *"I'm writing to follow up on the client proposal we submitted last Friday."*
2. **Walk through:** To guide or explain something step by step.  
   *"Could you walk us through the revised onboarding workflow during this morning's stand-up?"*
3. **Wrap up:** To bring an event or meeting to a successful conclusion.  
   *"Let's wrap up this discussion so everyone can get back to their sprint tasks."*
4. **Touch base (with):** To briefly make contact or confer with someone.  
   *"I'll touch base with the legal department this afternoon regarding the contract amendments."*
5. **Put off:** To postpone or delay an appointment or deadline.  
   *"We had to put off the launch date until the server migration is fully verified."*
6. **Call off:** To cancel an event entirely.  
   *"Management decided to call off the quarterly offsite due to budget constraints."*
7. **Pencil in:** To tentatively schedule an appointment.  
   *"Let's pencil in the strategy review for next Tuesday at two, subject to room availability."*
8. **Bring up:** To raise a topic or issue for discussion.  
   *"Marcus brought up several valid concerns regarding data privacy during the sprint demo."*

### Category 2: Problem Solving and Negotiation
9. **Sort out:** To organize, clarify, or resolve a confusing problem.  
   *"Our IT team is currently working to sort out the database permissions error."*
10. **Iron out:** To resolve small remaining disagreements or difficulties.  
    *"The commercial terms are agreed; we just need to iron out a few minor details in the service agreement."*
11. **Look into:** To investigate or examine a matter thoroughly.  
    *"Our customer support manager promised to look into the billing discrepancy immediately."*
12. **Come up against:** To encounter an unexpected obstacle or resistance.  
    *"We've come up against strict municipal zoning regulations that may delay site construction."*
13. **Deal with:** To take action to manage or solve a problem.  
    *"She has handled demanding enterprise clients with exceptional professionalism."*
14. **Rule out:** To exclude a possibility or course of action.  
    *"We cannot rule out the possibility of expanding into regional retail markets next year."*
15. **Back out (of):** To withdraw from an agreed commitment or deal.  
    *"The primary investor backed out of the funding round at the eleventh hour."*

### Category 3: Execution, Planning, and Delivery
16. **Carry out:** To perform or execute a task, study, or procedure.  
    *"The quality assurance engineers carried out rigorous stress testing on the mobile application."*
17. **Roll out:** To officially launch or introduce a new product, system, or policy.  
    *"The HR division will roll out the new hybrid working policy across all regional hubs next month."*
18. **Phase out:** To gradually stop using or providing something over time.  
    *"The company plans to phase out legacy desktop software by the end of the second quarter."*
19. **Scale up:** To increase the size, amount, or production capacity of something.  
    *"To meet surging holiday demand, we need to scale up our fulfillment cloud infrastructure."*
20. **Zero in on:** To focus all attention on a specific target or problem.  
    *"During the retrospectives, we zeroed in on three bottlenecks in our deployment pipeline."*
21. **Sign off on:** To give official approval or authorization to something.  
    *"The Chief Financial Officer has officially signed off on the revised capital expenditure budget."*
22. **Draw up:** To draft or prepare a formal document, contract, or plan.  
    *"Our external corporate solicitors are drawing up the non-disclosure agreement."*

### Category 4: Teamwork, Workload, and Communication
23. **Follow through (on):** To complete an action that was previously initiated or promised.  
    *"A major reason for her rapid promotion is that she consistently follows through on client commitments."*
24. **Take on:** To accept new work, responsibilities, or employees.  
    *"Our design agency is operating at full capacity and cannot take on any new external accounts this month."*
25. **Hand over:** To transfer responsibility or operational control to someone else.  
    *"Before leaving for paternity leave, he handed over his client portfolio to Sarah."*
26. **Fill in for:** To temporarily perform someone else's duties while they are absent.  
    *"Could you fill in for me at the weekly stakeholder sync while I'm at the conference?"*
27. **Fall behind (on):** To fail to keep pace with an agreed schedule or workload.  
    *"Due to repeated network outages, the development team has fallen behind on the quarterly milestone."*
28. **Catch up (on):** To do what is necessary to reach the required standard or timeline.  
    *"I plan to spend Friday morning catching up on administrative documentation and emails."*
29. **Burn out:** To become physically or mentally exhausted through prolonged workplace overwork.  
    *"Managers must actively monitor sprint loads to ensure engineers do not burn out."*
30. **Brush up on:** To refresh or improve one's knowledge of something previously learned.  
    *"Before the client presentation in Frankfurt, I spent the weekend brushing up on international compliance rules."*

## Separable vs inseparable phrasal verbs in business English

A common grammatical trap involves the placement of direct object pronouns (*it, them*):

- **Separable Phrasal Verbs:** The object can sit between the verb and particle. If the object is a pronoun (*it, them*), it **must** go in the middle:
  - *Correct:* *"I will **sort it out** before lunch."*
  - *Incorrect:* *"I will sort out it before lunch."*
  - Other separable verbs include: *call off, put off, walk through, roll out, draw up*.
- **Inseparable Phrasal Verbs:** The verb and particle must remain together, regardless of whether the object is a noun or a pronoun:
  - *Correct:* *"We must **look into it** immediately."*
  - *Incorrect:* *"We must look it into."*
  - Other inseparable verbs include: *come across, deal with, run into, fall behind on*.

## Self-check: business phrasal verb mastery

1. What phrasal verb means to delay a meeting to a future date?
2. Complete the sentence with the correct pronoun order: *"The contract has a few errors; let's sort (it / out)."*
3. Which phrasal verb means to explain a complex document step by step?
4. What is the difference between *taking on* work and *handing over* work?

**Answers**
1. **Put off** (or **push back**).
2. **sort it out** (*it* must sit in the middle of a separable phrasal verb).
3. **Walk through** (*walk someone through something*).
4. *Taking on* work means accepting new responsibility, while *handing over* work means transferring responsibility to another person.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are phrasal verbs considered too informal for business writing?

No. While ultra-formal legal contracts prefer single-word Latinate verbs (*postpone*, *terminate*), modern business communication across emails, memos, and meetings relies extensively on phrasal verbs like *follow up*, *carry out*, and *roll out*.

### How can I remember whether a phrasal verb is separable?

Practise the phrasal verb with the pronoun *it* repeatedly (*sort it out, wrap it up, call it off*). Your ear will quickly develop an intuitive sense for the natural acoustic rhythm.

### Can I use business phrasal verbs in IELTS Writing Task 2?

In formal IELTS academic essays, prefer single Latinate verbs (*investigate* instead of *look into*, *eliminate* instead of *get rid of*). However, in IELTS Speaking and General Training Task 1 letters, appropriate phrasal verbs demonstrate natural idiomatic command.
