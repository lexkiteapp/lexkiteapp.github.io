If you are a skilled worker or an international graduate applying for permanent residence in Canada, your language result is one of the biggest levers you control in the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS). The CELPIP vs IELTS for Canada choice comes down to two tests: CELPIP General or IELTS General Training.

Short answer: both give the same CRS points at the same CLB level. Pick CELPIP if you want an all-computer test with Canadian workplace tasks; pick IELTS if you want a live examiner or the option of a paper test.

Both tests are approved by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada for Express Entry. The [IRCC language test page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) lists CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training and PTE Core (this article compares the first two), and it says you must take the General Training version of IELTS. IRCC does not accept IELTS One Skill Retake, and your result must be less than two years old both when you complete your profile and when you apply. Other programs can have their own rules, so check the page for your stream.

## CELPIP vs IELTS for Canada, and what actually separates them

The two tests differ in several ways that matter for your choice:

{{diagram: d1 | Detailed comparison of CELPIP General and IELTS General Training.}}

### 1. CELPIP levels sit close to CLB numbers
One practical advantage of CELPIP is that its score scale is built around the **Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB)**:
- A score of **9 in CELPIP** equals **CLB 9**.
- A score of **7 in CELPIP** equals **CLB 7**.

IRCC shows CELPIP 10 and above as CLB 10, so there is no extra Express Entry credit above level 10.

In contrast, IELTS uses Band 0 to 9.0 and IRCC converts each band with its own table. For example, CLB 9 in IELTS means Listening 8.0 and Reading, Writing and Speaking 7.0. For a thorough breakdown of these conversions, review our [IELTS to CLB conversion guide](/en/blog/ielts-clb-score-canada/).

## The Speaking test: computer prompts vs human examiner

Speaking is where the two tests feel most different:

### CELPIP Speaking (100% Computer-Delivered)
You sit at a computer workstation, wearing a headset. Prompts appear on screen (e.g., describing an illustration, giving advice to a friend, expressing an opinion on a workplace policy). A digital timer counts down your preparation time, and then you speak into the microphone. CELPIP Speaking has eight tasks, each with its own preparation and speaking time; celpip.ca lists the current timings.
- **Advantages:** No human intimidation, and consistent automated timing.
- **Disadvantages:** Rigid time limits and no human reaction while you speak, so you have to keep going on your own.

### IELTS Speaking (Face-to-Face Interview)
You sit in a private, quiet room with a certified human examiner for 11 to 14 minutes.
- **Advantages:** Natural conversational pacing, human non-verbal cues, examiner can repeat or rephrase questions in Part 3.
- **Disadvantages:** Potential examiner subjectivity, nervousness from sitting directly across from an evaluator.

## The Writing section: everyday practical tasks vs academic essays

The two Writing papers ask for different kinds of text:

1. **CELPIP Writing (check celpip.ca for the current timing):**
   - **Task 1 (Email):** Writing an everyday functional email to a colleague, business, or local official.
   - **Task 2 (Responding to an Opinion Survey):** Stating and justifying a preference regarding an organizational dilemma, such as choosing between building a company cafeteria versus subsidizing gym memberships.

2. **IELTS General Writing (60 minutes):**
   - **Task 1 (Letter):** Writing a formal, semi-formal, or personal letter (150 words minimum). Explore our model letter breakdowns in our [General Training letter guide](/en/blog/ielts-general-training-letter/) and the [IELTS Writing model answers hub](/en/samples/).
   - **Task 2 (Discursive Essay):** Composing a formal argumentative essay (250 words minimum) addressing broader social or environmental themes.

If timed academic-style essays worry you, CELPIP's email and survey tasks may feel more practical.

## How Reading and Listening work on screen

The papers also look different on screen:

- **CELPIP Reading & Listening:** All answers are selected from options on screen, so you never type answers or lose marks on spelling in Reading and Listening.
- **IELTS Reading & Listening:** Uses many question types, including sentence completion, matching headings, True/False/Not Given, and diagram labelling. Questions are visible in advance, allowing you to preview keywords before listening begins. Review our targeted techniques for [matching headings in IELTS Reading](/en/blog/ielts-reading-matching-headings/) to master these varied task structures.

## Which test should you select for Canadian Express Entry?

Consider your personal strengths and testing preferences:

### Choose CELPIP General if:
1. You are already living, working, or studying in North America and are comfortable with North American English.
2. You type quickly and comfortably.
3. You prefer structured everyday workplace writing (emails and surveys) over 250-word formal essays.
4. You feel comfortable speaking into a microphone with on-screen visual prompts.

### Choose IELTS General Training if:
1. You feel more confident communicating with a live human being during speaking assessments.
2. You live outside Canada and no CELPIP centre is within reach (check celpip.ca).
3. You are also considering other countries. See [IELTS score for Australia PR](/en/blog/ielts-score-australia-pr/) and [IELTS score for New Zealand residence](/en/blog/ielts-new-zealand-skilled-migrant/).
4. You prefer having the option of paper-based test delivery.

One caution for that third point: for UK visas that need a Secure English Language Test, IELTS for UKVI (a separate in-person test) is one of several approved tests, and Australia's Home Affairs table now lists CELPIP General too, so compare the current tables.

For broader visa context, see our [IELTS for immigration overview](/en/blog/ielts-for-immigration-guide/).

### A five-question checklist before you book
Answer each with yes or no; any "no" is a reason to check the official page first.
- Is your stream's language-test page showing CELPIP-General or IELTS General Training (not Academic)?
- Is the test you picked one that IRCC accepts as a whole result? IRCC does not accept IELTS One Skill Retake.
- Will the result still be less than two years old on the day you complete your profile and on the day you apply?
- Is a test centre for your choice within reach? Check celpip.ca for CELPIP and the booking page for IELTS.
- Did you pick the test whose Speaking and Writing format you can repeat calmly under time pressure?

## A worked example: reading a score report against the table
The two reports below are invented for illustration, not real results.

**IELTS General Training report:** Listening 8.0, Reading 7.0, Writing 6.5, Speaking 7.0. IRCC's conversion for CLB 9 is Listening 8.0 and Reading, Writing and Speaking 7.0. Listening, Reading and Speaking reach it, but Writing 6.5 is below 7.0, so this report does not reach CLB 9 in Writing. The next step is to look up in IRCC's table which CLB level Writing 6.5 gives, and to decide whether to retake the whole test (IRCC does not accept One Skill Retake) or to practise Writing before a new sitting.

**CELPIP-General report:** Listening 12, Reading 10, Writing 9, Speaking 10. IRCC shows levels 10 to 12 as CLB 10, so the 12 in Listening gives no more than a 10 would. The 9 in Writing is the number that holds the report back if you are aiming for CLB 10 in every skill.

The habit to copy is the same in both cases: read each of the four skills separately, find the lowest one, and compare it with the official table, not with your average.

## General language proficiency vs test-specific tricks

Memorised templates will not bridge the gap to CLB 9 or 10. Both tests rate genuine grammatical control, vocabulary range and clarity.

Prioritise daily vocabulary acquisition, listening comprehension across diverse registers, and clear paragraph structuring. Developing sustained study habits—detailed in our guide on [how to build daily English habits for IELTS](/en/blog/daily-english-habits-for-ielts/)—keeps your English steady whichever test you sit.

## Self-check: comparing CELPIP and IELTS

1. What Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) level corresponds to a score of 9 in CELPIP?
2. Which test permits a face-to-face speaking interview with a human examiner?
3. What are the two tasks evaluated in CELPIP Writing?
4. Is CELPIP General accepted for Australian skilled migration?

**Answers**
1. **CLB 9** (CELPIP levels 4 to 9 map one to one to CLB).
2. **IELTS.**
3. **Writing an email** (Task 1) and **responding to a survey question** (Task 2).
4. **Yes, in the in-person CELPIP General version.** The Department of Home Affairs lists CELPIP General in its table for tests taken from 7 August 2025 (the older table does not include it, and CELPIP Online is not accepted), so compare the current score table before you choose. For the UK and New Zealand, check each official page for the tests it accepts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does IRCC prefer CELPIP or IELTS for Express Entry?

Both tests earn the same Comprehensive Ranking System points when your results reach the same CLB level, so the choice is about which format suits you.

### Can I take CELPIP outside of Canada?

Yes. Paragon Testing Enterprises runs CELPIP at test centres outside Canada as well. Check the current list of locations on celpip.ca before you pay, because it changes.

### How long are CELPIP results valid for Canadian immigration?

IRCC wants the result to be less than two years old both when you complete your Express Entry profile and when you submit your permanent residence application, for CELPIP and IELTS alike.
