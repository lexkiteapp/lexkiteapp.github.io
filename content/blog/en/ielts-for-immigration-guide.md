The right **IELTS for immigration** plan starts with three questions, in this order: which version of IELTS does your visa accept, what score does your exact programme ask for in each skill, and will your result still be valid on the day you apply? The first question is where plans most often go wrong. People prepare for months for Academic when their application needed General Training, or book a standard test when the UK needed a Secure English Language Test. This guide shows how to check each point on the official pages, what the main destinations asked for when we checked on 5 November 2026, and how to turn a target into a study plan.

A note before we start: this is language-test guidance, not legal advice, and no one can promise a visa outcome. Rules change often, so treat every number below as a snapshot and confirm it on the official page before you pay for a test.

## Step 1: Choose the right IELTS for immigration

IELTS comes in three forms that matter for migration.

**IELTS General Training** has the same Listening and Speaking as Academic, but Reading uses everyday and workplace texts and Writing Task 1 is a letter instead of a graph description. Task 2 is an essay in both versions. Canada's Express Entry accepts only this version.

**IELTS Academic** uses academic texts in Reading and asks you to describe a chart, table or process in Writing Task 1. It is built for university entry, and some migration systems accept it, but you must check before choosing.

**IELTS for UKVI** is the version run for the UK Home Office as a Secure English Language Test (SELT). It is taken only at approved centres. Its content follows the standard test, but the booking, the result form and the UKVI reference number are different. If you take a standard IELTS for a UK application that needs a SELT, the result will not count.

The simple rule: never book until you have found the sentence on the official page that names the test. A cheap way to avoid doubt is to copy that sentence into your notes together with the page address and date.

## Step 2: What the official sources said on 5 November 2026

The table above summarises it. Here is the detail, with the one thing you must remember for each place.

### Canada: IELTS General Training and CLB

The [IRCC Express Entry language test page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents/language-test.html) says that, for Express Entry, IELTS is accepted only as **IELTS General Training**, and that IELTS One Skill Retake is not accepted. Results must be less than two years old both when you complete your profile and when you submit your permanent residence application. Expired results lead to refusal, so a test that was valid in January can become useless by the time an invitation arrives.

Canada converts your scores into Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) levels per skill. For General Training, IRCC's conversion table gave these IELTS scores:

| CLB | Speaking | Listening | Reading | Writing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 |
| 8 | 6.5 | 7.5 | 6.5 | 6.5 |
| 9 | 7.0 | 8.0 | 7.0 | 7.0 |

The full chart, from CLB 4 to CLB 10, is in our [IELTS to CLB conversion](/en/blog/ielts-clb-score-canada/) guide. Notice that the thresholds are not the same across skills. Reaching CLB 9 asks for 8.0 in Listening but 7.0 in the others, so one balanced "7.5" profile does not automatically give you the same CLB in every skill. The Federal Skilled Worker page on the same site lists CLB 7 as the lowest level that qualifies in the first-language points table; other programmes (trades, Canadian Experience Class, provincial streams) set their own minimums, so read the one you are applying for.

### The UK: IELTS for UKVI and CEFR B2

The [GOV.UK Skilled Worker English page](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/knowledge-of-english) says you must prove English to at least CEFR level **B2** in reading, writing, speaking and listening. The exception noted on the page covers people who held the visa before 8 January 2026 and are extending or updating it, who need B1. You can prove English through a UK school qualification, a degree taught in English with the right assessment, or a Secure English Language Test. Nationals of a list of majority English-speaking countries, which includes Canada, Australia, New Zealand and the USA, are exempt. Doctors, dentists, nurses, midwives and vets who have passed an assessment accepted by their professional body do not need to prove it again.

The [SELT guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) names **IELTS for UKVI** as an approved test, says you can only take it with an approved provider at an approved centre, and says results are valid for two years from the award date. The level and the version (Academic or General Training) depend on your route, so open the guidance for your visa and the approved-tests list. Partner, parent, settlement and citizenship applications need a test of Speaking and Listening only, which changes which test you book.

### Australia: levels by skill

The Department of Home Affairs describes English ability in levels: competent, proficient and superior. For IELTS taken from 7 August 2025, the department's level pages list 6, 7 and 8 in **each** of the four components for those levels. The department's own example says the lowest component sets your level, even if the overall band looks higher.

Tests taken from 7 August 2025 follow new rules, and results are generally accepted if achieved within the three years before the visa application, depending on the subclass. Tests delivered completely online, including IELTS Online, are not accepted. Because points and the required level differ between visas, open the [Home Affairs English language page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-support/meeting-our-requirements/english-language) and your subclass page together before you plan. The department's site blocked our automated check on 5 November 2026, so these Australian figures come from its published page summaries: confirm them on the live pages before you book.

### New Zealand: 6.5 for the principal applicant

The [Immigration New Zealand page for skilled residence visas](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/process-to-apply/applying-for-a-visa/providing-evidence-and-documents-to-support-your-visa-application/english-language-requirements/english-language-requirements-for-skilled-residence-visas/) gave an overall IELTS score of **6.5 or more** for the principal applicant, in the General or Academic version, with One Skill Retake accepted. The test must be taken in person at a test centre, and the result must be no more than two years old when you apply. Immigration New Zealand also published amendment circulars in 2026 that revise the English-language instructions, so check the age limit and tests on the current page before you rely on this paragraph.

### The USA: ask what your form says

Most United States immigration categories do not ask for an IELTS band score the way the destinations above do. English is typically assessed inside the specific process, for example a naturalization interview. If a US employer, university or licensing body asks for IELTS, they will tell you which version and band; follow their instructions and the form instructions on uscis.gov for your category.

## Step 3: Check validity and the one-sitting trap

Two things quietly ruin applications.

**Validity.** Most systems above looked at the test date, not the date you booked. Two years is common (Canada, the UK, New Zealand), and Australia usually looks back up to three years depending on the visa. Count backward from the date you expect to apply, not forward from the date of the test. If you are waiting for an invitation, plan a retest window.

**Combining results.** Some systems allow one skill to be retaken; Canada does not accept IELTS One Skill Retake for Express Entry. Others may accept it, so never assume. If you are one band short in one skill, the exact retake rule of your programme decides whether you spend one skill's fee or a full test.

## Step 4: Turn the requirement into a target

Write your target as four numbers, not one: Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking. Then write your current level for each. The gap that matters is almost always one skill, not the average.

| Skill | Target from official page | Current level | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening | | | |
| Reading | | | |
| Writing | | | |
| Speaking | | | |

Fill the first column from the page for your own programme and leave the rest to an honest practice test. Be careful with a single online "band predictor": it estimates, it does not give an official result, and the real examiner marking is the only score that counts. A short, timed mock test under realistic conditions is a better starting point than any quiz. Our guide to [how to break an IELTS band plateau](/en/blog/how-to-break-ielts-band-plateau/) explains how to find the skill that holds you back.

Then match time to gap. A half-band gap in Listening usually needs different work from a half-band gap in Writing: Listening improves with daily short dictation and question-type practice, Writing improves with feedback on full responses. If you have 12 weeks, a common pattern is to spend the first three on diagnosing and basics, the middle six on your two weakest skills, and the last three on full timed practice. If you have six months, be more patient with vocabulary and grammar range. Our [IELTS self-study plan](/en/blog/ielts-self-study-plan/) lays out a week-by-week road map you can adapt.

## What changes if you choose General Training

If your programme needs General Training, shift part of your prep towards the tasks that Academic candidates never see. In Writing, Task 1 is a letter, and tone is scored as much as content: complaining to a landlord needs a different register from thanking a colleague. Our full guide to the [IELTS General Training letter](/en/blog/ielts-general-training-letter/) covers formal, semi-formal and informal letters with common mistakes. In Reading, you will meet notices, adverts and workplace documents, so practise scanning for details quickly.

For Task 2, the essay structure is shared with Academic, so the [IELTS Writing Task 2 structure](/en/blog/ielts-writing-task-2-structure/) guide applies to you too. For Speaking, which has the same three parts in every version, [Part 2 tips](/en/blog/ielts-speaking-part-2-tips/) are worth reading early, because many migration candidates lose marks on fluency under time pressure rather than on difficult vocabulary.

## Self-check

1. A Canadian Express Entry applicant has an IELTS Academic result with 7.0 in each skill. Is it accepted?
2. In which order should you check things before booking a test?
3. A UK skilled worker route needs a SELT. Which IELTS product is named on GOV.UK's SELT page?
4. An Australian applicant has Listening 8.0, Reading 8.0, Writing 8.0 and Speaking 6.5. Does the profile reach proficient English (7 in each component, on the pages we reviewed)?
5. A result is dated 3 March 2025. Using a two-year rule, until roughly when can it be used?

### Answers

1. **No.** The IRCC page says IELTS is accepted only as General Training for Express Entry. Academic does not count there.
2. **Test version, then score per skill, then validity.** Booking first and checking later is how people end up retesting.
3. **IELTS for UKVI.** A standard IELTS result is not a SELT.
4. **No.** The lowest component sets the level, so Speaking 6.5 holds the profile at competent (6) even though three skills are at 8.0.
5. **Until about 3 March 2027,** and a test valid at lodging time is what matters, so plan your date from the end.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is IELTS General Training easier than Academic?

It is different, not automatically easier. The texts in Reading are more everyday, and the Task 1 letter is simpler in content than describing a chart. But the band scale is the same, and Writing and Speaking are marked with the same four criteria. Choose the version your visa requires, not the one that feels easier.

### How long is an IELTS result valid for immigration?

It depends on the system. Canada, the UK and New Zealand were using two years on the pages we checked, and Australia generally used a longer look-back that depends on the visa. Always count from the date you expect to submit the application.

### Can I use IELTS Online or a home edition?

Do not assume you can. Australia said fully remote-proctored tests were not accepted, and New Zealand required an in-person sitting. The UK needs a SELT at an approved centre. Check the page for your visa before booking.

### Do I need a separate test for each country?

Not always. A single IELTS result might satisfy more than one system if the version and scores fit and the result is still valid for each. But rules differ, for example General Training only for Express Entry, so confirm each one separately.
