sticky_note_2 Tense Notes for Adult Candidates

• Dynamic Verb: Unlike stative verbs (know, believe), go readily forms progressive continuous aspects across all registers.

• Compound Futurity: be going to + infinitive acts as a modal-like future semi-auxiliary expressing prior intention or immediate perceptual evidence.

error_outline

Common Learner Mistakes

IELTS Band 5–7 traps
cancel I have went to London twice.
check_circle I have gone to London / I have been to London.

Rule: Never use V2 (went) after auxiliary have/has/had. The past participle V3 is gone (or been for completed round-trips).

cancel After the lecture I will go to home.
check_circle After the lecture I will go home.

Rule: Home functions adverbially here; omit preposition to.

cancel He has gone to Paris and is back now.
check_circle He has been to Paris and is back now.

Rule: Has gone means still there / en route. Has been indicates a completed round journey.

compare_arrows

Often Confused With

Directional pairs
go vs come

go: Movement away from speaker / current anchor.

come: Movement toward the speaker / listener.

"I must go now" / "Come here"
go vs went

go: Present/infinitive. Used after modal verbs: can go, must go.

went: Past only. Never say *did went or *must went.

"Did you go?" (not *went)
