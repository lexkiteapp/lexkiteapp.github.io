Meanings & Examples

1 Psychological • Figurative

Able to quickly become strong, healthy, or happy again after illness, shock, or difficult circumstances.

"Children are often remarkably resilient in the face of emotional changes."

2 Materials & Economics

Able to return to an original shape or stability after being bent, stretched, or pressured.

"Building a resilient local economy requires investing in sustainable energy."

Easily Confused: Resilient vs. Resistant

replay Resilient (Bounces Back)

Absorbs damage, changes, or shocks, but quickly recovers to full strength.

e.g. "A resilient mindset after exam setbacks."
shield Resistant (Blocks Off)

Prevents something from affecting it in the first place; does not yield.

e.g. "Water-resistant watch; resistant to heat."
tips_and_updates Examiner Pro Tip

“In IELTS Speaking Part 3, swap generic adjectives like 'strong' or 'tough' for 'resilient'. Examiners reward precision when you discuss environmental rebuilding or career adaptability.”
