The history of the bicycle

A • Early two-wheeled conveyances, such as the German Draisine invented by Karl Drais in 1817, lacked mechanical pedals and required riders to propel themselves by pushing their feet against the ground. These wooden devices became popular recreational novelties across western European capitals before being banned on pedestrian walkways.

B • The introduction of pedals attached directly to the front wheel hub created the "velocipede" in 1860s Paris. Subsequent developments in chain drives and equal-sized pneumatic tires during the late 1880s transformed cycling from an athletic luxury into an affordable, ubiquitous mode of everyday urban transport.
