Present perfect vs past simple

Use past simple when the time door is closed and finished; use present perfect when the experience still touches right now.

💡
Golden rule: Specific timestamp in the past? Drop "have" immediately.
01

When to use: The Mental Picture

Core Concept

Think of tenses not as formulas to memorize, but as cameras focusing on time. Both discuss actions before this exact second, but their cameras are pointed in completely opposite directions:

Action in a closed box Historic

Past Simple

The event started and completely finished at an identified time. The period is over, and the door to that chapter is shut.

Dead giveaways (Time markers):
yesterday in 2021 3 weeks ago last night when I was young
“I lived in Tokyo for two years.” (I don't live there anymore)
Connected to right now Life experience

Present Perfect

The action happened at an unspecified moment in life, or continues up to today, or creates a direct consequence on this current minute.

Dead giveaways (Connectors):
already yet since Monday for 5 years ever / never so far
“I have lived in Tokyo for two years.” (I still live there today)
☕

Tutor Observation: The Broken Key Test

If you tell someone “I lost my keys” (Past Simple), you are just recounting history; maybe you found them an hour later. But if you say “I have lost my keys!” (Present Perfect), it means you are standing outside your door right now with no way to get in. The consequence lives in the present.

02

Structure & Form Reference

Syntax Guide

Compare the structural anatomy of both tenses side-by-side across positive, negative, and interrogative sentences:

Sentence Type Past Simple (V2 / did) Present Perfect (have/has + V3)
(+) Positive Subject + V2 (past form) “She finished her master’s thesis yesterday.” Subject + have/has + V3 (past participle) “She has finished her master’s thesis.”
(−) Negative Subject + did not + base verb “They did not meet the deadline last Friday.” Subject + have/has not + V3 “They have not met the deadline yet.”
(?) Question Did + Subject + base verb? “Did you see the manager this morning?” Have/Has + Subject + V3? “Have you seen the manager today?”
★ Memory latch: With did in past simple negatives/questions, the main verb reverts to base form (did you eat, not did you ate).
03

Real Sentences in Academic & Daily Context

Contextual Pairs

Study how subtle shifts in nuance dramatically alter meaning in essays and interviews:

Scenario A: Scientific breakthroughs & history
Past Simple (Finished milestone)

“Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928.”

Specific year stated: 1928 is firmly closed history.
Present Perfect (Ongoing field)

“Researchers have discovered several new antibiotics in recent years.”

Unspecified time range continuing into our modern era.
Scenario B: Career experience in IELTS Speaking
Past Simple (Prior position)

“I worked as a graphic designer before starting university.”

The job has ended; the speaker is now a student.
Present Perfect (Current profession)

“I have worked in tech for over six years.”

The speaker began 6 years ago and still works in tech today.
Scenario C: "Today" / Time period still open
Past Simple (Spoken at 9:00 PM)

“I drank three cups of coffee this morning.”

The morning has passed; the coffee drinking is over.
Present Perfect (Spoken at 10:30 AM)

“I have drunk two cups of coffee this morning.”

It is still morning; the speaker might brew another cup!
04

Exam Pitfalls: Wrong → Right Pairs

Band 7 Fixes

These are the 3 most frequent tense blunders examiners penalize in IELTS Task 2 essays and Speaking cues:

Trap 1: Mixing present perfect with exact past timestamps
✕ WRONG: “I have seen that documentary last night.”
✓ RIGHT: “I saw that documentary last night.”

Why? Words like yesterday, last night, in 2019, when I arrived anchor the action to a closed time window. Present perfect is strictly forbidden with fixed past timestamps.

Trap 2: Confusing "since" (start point) and "for" (duration)
✕ WRONG: “Global emissions increased since 1990.”
✓ RIGHT: “Global emissions have increased since 1990.”

Why? The word since bridges the starting year (1990) straight into today. Because the timeline is still stretching forward, present perfect is required.

Trap 3: Using past simple for uncompleted life experiences
✕ WRONG: “Did you ever visit London?”
✓ RIGHT: “Have you ever visited London?”

Why? Unless the person is deceased, their lifespan is still active. Questions inquiring about life experience before now use Have you ever...

05

3-Question Mini Check

Interactive Drill

Test your instinct before moving to the next drill. Select your answer, then click “Reveal Explanation”:

Question 1 of 3 Single Choice

1. "Dr. Adams ______ his findings at the symposium yesterday afternoon."

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: B) presented Because “yesterday afternoon” is an explicit past time anchor, past simple is strictly required. Present perfect cannot take past timestamps.
Question 2 of 3 Single Choice

2. "I can't open my email account because I ______ my login credentials."

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: A) have forgotten The present result is prominent: “I can't open my email account [right now]”. The past forgetting directly causes the present blockage, demanding Present Perfect.
Question 3 of 3 Single Choice

3. "How long ______ in Hanoi before you relocated to Melbourne?"

Reveal Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: B) did you live The living period in Hanoi is completely terminated because the speaker relocated to Melbourne. Use Past Simple for completed durations in past chapters.
