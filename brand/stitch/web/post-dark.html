What is the Academic Word List (AWL)?

Unlike general high-frequency English lists (such as the General Service List), the Academic Word List comprises 570 word families frequently found across diverse academic disciplines—from organic chemistry and macroeconomics to developmental psychology.

The list excludes general vocabulary found in everyday speech (such as water, house, or time) as well as hyperspecialized niche terms (like mitochondria). Instead, it pinpoints the analytical scaffolding that examiners evaluate under the Lexical Resource rubric in Writing Task 2 and Speaking Part 3.

Lemma Verification

"The researcher conducted a comprehensive ______ of the clinical trial data."

info
Correct answer is B (analysis). The slot directly follows the qualifying adjective comprehensive and article a, mandating the singular noun derivative rather than the verb (analyze) or adjective (analytical).
Common Traps

Mistakes to Avoid with AWL

warning

Nominalization Overload: Packing every clause with abstract nouns ("The realization of the implementation of...") destroys cohesion and penalizes grammatical range. Strive for balanced active verb structures.

error

Faulty Preposition Collocations: Memorizing the word without its dependent preposition is hazardous. Never write "attribute on" (correct: attribute to) or "consistent on" (correct: consistent with).

Frequently Asked Questions

How many AWL words should I study every day for IELTS? keyboard_arrow_down
Aim for 10 to 15 word families per day, never more. Studying 15 lemmas along with their full derivative trees (verb, noun, adjective, adverb) equals roughly 50 new lexical units. At this pace, you can cover all 570 families thoroughly in approximately 5 to 6 weeks.
Does memorizing the AWL guarantee an IELTS 7.5 Band in Writing? keyboard_arrow_down
No single list guarantees a band score. Lexical Resource accounts for 25% of your Writing Task 2 score. You must also satisfy Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, and Grammatical Range. However, precise AWL deployment is almost universally present in scripts scoring 7.5 and above.
