biodegradable

adjective B2 / C1
/ˌbaɪ.əʊ.dɪˈɡreɪ.də.bəl/ UK · US
Definition

Capable of being decomposed by bacteria or other biological means, avoiding permanent chemical pollution.

Academic Context

“Replacing conventional single-use plastics with certified biodegradable polymers would significantly alleviate the accumulation of microplastics in aquatic habitats.”

ecosystem

noun B2
/ˈiː.kəʊˌsɪs.təm/ UK · US
Definition

All living organisms in an area and the non-living physical components with which they continuously interact.

Academic Context

“Overfishing disrupts the delicate equilibrium of marine ecosystems, triggering trophic cascades that diminish predatory fish stocks.”

deforestation

noun B2 / C1
/diːˌfɒr.ɪˈsteɪ.ʃən/ UK · US
Definition

The permanent clearance and destruction of vast forest tracts to make land available for agriculture or urbanisation.

Academic Context

“Unchecked tropical deforestation releases immense reserves of sequestered carbon back into the atmosphere.”

renewable

adjective B1 / B2
/rɪˈnjuː.ə.bəl/ UK · US
Definition

Relating to energy sources that are not depleted by use, such as solar, wind, tidal, or geothermal power.

Academic Context

“Subsidising photovoltaic and wind infrastructure enables emerging nations to transition toward renewable energy without sacrificing industrial growth.”

conservation

noun B2 / C1
/ˌkɒn.səˈveɪ.ʃən/ UK · US
Definition

The careful protection, management, and preservation of natural wildlife, habitats, and ecological resources.

Academic Context

“Community-led wildlife conservation initiatives have proven considerably more effective than enforcement-only park boundaries.”

contaminant

noun C1
/kənˈtæm.ɪ.nənt/ UK · US
Definition

A polluting substance that makes water, air, or soil impure, toxic, or hazardous to living organisms.

Academic Context

“Agricultural runoff introduces toxic chemical contaminants into groundwater tables, complicating municipal filtration procedures.”
