Present perfect vs past simple

Use past simple when the time door is closed and finished; use present perfect when the experience still touches right now.

lightbulb
Golden Rule

Specific time stamp in the past? Drop 'have' immediately.

01 CORE CONCEPT

When to use: The Mental Picture

Think of tenses not as formulas to memorize, but as cameras focusing on time. Both discuss actions before this exact second, but their cameras are pointed in completely opposite directions:

Action in a Closed Box • Historic

Past Simple

The event belongs to a historical segment that is over. The time window has sealed shut behind it.

DEAD GIVEAWAYS (TIME MARKERS)
yesterday in 2021 3 weeks ago last night when I was young

“I lived in Tokyo for two years.”

lock (I don't live there anymore)

Connected to Right Now • Life experience

Present Perfect

The event has a lifeline attached to the present moment. The experience or result is active right now.

DEAD GIVEAWAYS (CONNECTORS)
already yet since Monday for 5 years ever / never so far

“I have lived in Tokyo for two years.”

check_circle (I still live there today)

local_cafe
TUTOR OBSERVATION: THE BROKEN KEY TEST

If you lost your keys 10 minutes ago and cannot enter your house right now, say “I have lost my key” (current problem). But if you found them an hour later, you must say “I lost my key this morning” because the consequence is resolved.

02 SYNTAX GUIDE

Structure & Form Reference

Compare the structural anatomy of both tenses side-by-side across positive, negative, and interrogative sentences:

SENTENCE TYPE PAST SIMPLE (V2 / DID) PRESENT PERFECT (HAVE/HAS + V3)
+ Positive Subject + V2 “They published the paper in May.” Subject + have/has + V3 “They have published three papers this year.”
- Negative Subject + did not + base verb “We did not receive approval yesterday.” Subject + have/has not + V3 “We have not received approval yet.”
? Question Did + Subject + base verb? “Did you finish the lab report?” Have/Has + Subject + V3? “Have you ever analyzed this dataset?”
bookmark Memory latch: With 'did' in past simple negatives and questions, the main verb reverts to base form (did you eat, not did you ate).
03 CONTEXTUAL PAIRS

Real Sentences in Academic & Daily Context

Observe how native speakers and IELTS Band 8+ examinees swap tenses depending entirely on their time frame:

Scenario A: Scientific Breakthroughs & History HISTORICAL VS RECENT
PAST SIMPLE

“Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928.”

Closed event tied to a fixed historical date (1928).

PRESENT PERFECT

“Researchers have discovered dozens of antibiotic strains.”

Cumulative discovery with ongoing medical relevance today.

Scenario B: Career Experience in IELTS Speaking Part 1 & 2 JOB HISTORY
PAST SIMPLE

“I worked as a software intern last summer.”

The internship ended completely; last summer is over.

PRESENT PERFECT

“I have worked in tech for over five years.”

Career started 5 years ago and continues currently.

Scenario C: 'Today' / Time Period Still Open OPEN TIMELINE
PAST SIMPLE

“I drank two cups of coffee this morning.”

Spoken in the afternoon or evening when 'this morning' is gone.

PRESENT PERFECT

“I have drunk two cups of coffee this morning.”

Spoken at 10 AM while the morning window is still active.

04 BAND 7 FIXES

Exam Pitfalls: Wrong → Right Pairs

Examiners spot these three recurring grammatical mismatches instantly. Memorize these contrast patterns:

TRAP 1: MIXING PRESENT PERFECT WITH SPECIFIC TIMESTAMPS
WRONG

“I have completed my degree in 2022.”

RIGHT

“I completed my degree in 2022.”

Why: The specific point in time (in 2022) cuts any link to the present, requiring Past Simple immediately.

TRAP 2: CONFUSING 'SINCE' (START POINT) AND 'FOR' (DURATION)
WRONG

“She has studied law since four years.”

RIGHT

“She has studied law for four years.”

Why: Use for with an expanse or duration of time (4 years, 2 hours). Use since with an exact anchor point (since 2020, since March).

TRAP 3: USING PAST SIMPLE FOR UNCOMPLETED LIFE EXPERIENCES
WRONG

“Did you ever visit Canada?”

RIGHT

“Have you ever visited Canada?”

Why: When inquiring about general human lifetime experiences without a timeframe restriction, Present Perfect is essential.

05 INTERACTIVE DRILL

3-Question Mini Check

Test your instinct on authentic test items. Choose the correct verb form, then reveal the pedagogical reasoning:

QUESTION 1 • TIME ANCHOR

“The government _____ the public transport subsidy last November.”

chevron_right Reveal Answer & Explanation

Correct Answer: B. increased

The sentence gives a specific past timestamp ("last November"). Once a closed timeline is stated, you cannot use present perfect.

QUESTION 2 • CONTINUING ACTION

“Dr. Aris _____ over forty peer-reviewed journal papers so far.”

chevron_right Reveal Answer & Explanation

Correct Answer: A. has written

"So far" indicates that the action continues to the current moment and more publications may follow.

QUESTION 3 • RESULT NOW

“Sorry I can't open the PDF, I _____ my laptop password.”

chevron_right Reveal Answer & Explanation

Correct Answer: B. have forgotten

The past action directly affects current capability (can't open the file right now), demanding present perfect.
